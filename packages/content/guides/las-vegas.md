## Why visit Las Vegas

Las Vegas is built for entertainment. Themed mega-resorts line the Strip, each with its own restaurants, pools, shows and casinos, and the city has become a major destination for dining, concerts and sports. Beyond the neon, the Mojave Desert offers dramatic scenery within easy driving distance, from red rock canyons to Hoover Dam. Whether you gamble or not, there is plenty to fill a long weekend.

## Top sights and experiences

- **The Las Vegas Strip** — walk it at night to see the full spectacle of lights and architecture.
- **Fountains of Bellagio** — free choreographed water shows set to music.
- **Fremont Street Experience** — a canopy light show over the pedestrian heart of downtown.
- **Live shows** — acrobatic productions, magic, comedy and headliner residencies.
- **Red Rock Canyon National Conservation Area** — a scenic drive and hiking trails just west of the city.
- **Hoover Dam** — an engineering landmark on the Nevada–Arizona border.
- **The Neon Museum** — a "boneyard" of restored vintage signs from old Vegas.
- **Sphere** — a spherical venue with an immersive screen that has become a skyline landmark.
- **Pool season** — resort pools and dayclubs are a major draw in warm months.
- **Valley of Fire State Park** — vivid red sandstone formations about an hour away.

## Where to stay: best neighborhoods

**Center Strip** suits first-timers who want to walk to many resorts. Pros: maximum convenience, iconic properties. Cons: busy and often the most expensive.

**South Strip** works for travelers who want large resorts near the airport and arena. Pros: close to the airport, big pool complexes. Cons: longer walk to Center Strip.

**North Strip** fits visitors near the convention center. Pros: newer resorts, convention access. Cons: some stretches are less walkable.

**Downtown Las Vegas** appeals to budget-minded and nostalgia seekers. Pros: lower rates, vintage casinos, the Arts District nearby. Cons: a drive or bus ride from the Strip.

**Henderson** is good for a quieter, family-oriented stay. Pros: calmer resorts, close to Lake Mead. Cons: you will need a car.

## Things to know before you go

- Many hotels add a mandatory daily resort fee and may charge for parking; check the total cost before booking.
- Tipping is part of the culture: restaurant servers, bartenders, cocktail servers, valets, housekeeping and dealers are commonly tipped.
- Domestic travelers should confirm their ID meets current TSA rules; international visitors should check official sources for US entry requirements.
- Check official government travel advisories before your trip. Keep an eye on your belongings in crowded casinos and on busy sidewalks.
- People on the sidewalks may hand out cards or offer "free" show tickets or timeshare presentations; read the fine print and only buy tickets from official box offices or trusted sellers.
- The desert heat is serious in summer. Drink water often, and carry extra on hikes.
- You must be 21 to gamble and to drink alcohol.

## Getting around

The Strip looks compact but distances between resorts are longer than they appear. Walking, pedestrian bridges and free trams between some resorts help. The Las Vegas Monorail runs along the east side of the Strip, and the Deuce bus covers the Strip and downtown. Taxis and ride-hailing are plentiful, and the airport sits very close to the south end of the Strip. Rent a car for Red Rock, Valley of Fire or Hoover Dam.

## Best time to visit

Spring and fall bring warm, comfortable days. Summer is extremely hot but pool season is in full swing. Winter is cool and can be good value outside holidays and big events. Large conventions and sports weekends raise hotel rates sharply, so check the calendar before you book.

## Budget tips

- Midweek stays are often much cheaper than weekends.
- Compare the full price including resort fees, not just the nightly rate.
- Downtown and off-Strip hotels offer value if you do not mind a short ride.
- Enjoy free attractions like the Bellagio fountains, the Conservatory and Fremont Street.
- Look for same-day discount ticket booths for shows.
- Join resort loyalty programs, which can offer dining or room perks.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many days do I need in Las Vegas?

Two to four days is typical. Add a day or two if you want to visit Red Rock Canyon, Hoover Dam or take a longer trip toward the Grand Canyon.

### Is Las Vegas good for families?

Yes, though it takes planning. Many resorts have pools and family attractions, and nature trips outside the city appeal to all ages. Casino floors have age restrictions.

### Can I visit the Grand Canyon from Las Vegas?

Yes. The West Rim is the closest section, and the South Rim is a much longer drive best suited to an overnight trip. Tours and flights are also available.

### Do I need a car in Las Vegas?

Not for the Strip itself. A car is useful for day trips into the desert.
