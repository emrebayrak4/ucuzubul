## Why visit Los Angeles

Los Angeles is less a single city than a sprawling collection of neighborhoods, beach towns and hillsides tied together by freeways. It is the heart of the American film and TV industry, but it also offers serious art museums, an outstanding and diverse food scene, hiking trails with ocean views and miles of Pacific coastline. Mild weather most of the year makes it easy to mix outdoor time with city sightseeing.

## Top sights and experiences

- **Griffith Observatory** — free-entry views of the Hollywood Sign and the basin, best near sunset.
- **Santa Monica Pier and beach** — a classic seaside amusement pier and a long stretch of sand.
- **The Getty Center** — hilltop galleries, gardens and architecture with sweeping city views.
- **Hollywood Walk of Fame** — the star-studded sidewalk along Hollywood Boulevard.
- **Venice Beach Boardwalk** — street performers, skate park and the nearby canals.
- **Universal Studios Hollywood** — a theme park paired with a working studio tour.
- **LACMA and the Miracle Mile** — a major art museum near the La Brea Tar Pits.
- **The Broad** — a contemporary art museum in Downtown with timed entry.
- **Hiking in Runyon Canyon or the Santa Monica Mountains** — quick escapes into nature.
- **Grand Central Market and Koreatown** — two of many great places to eat your way across the city.

## Where to stay: best neighborhoods

**Santa Monica** suits beach lovers and families. Pros: walkable, ocean breezes, rail link to Downtown. Cons: hotels can be expensive and far from eastside sights.

**Hollywood** works for first-timers focused on landmarks. Pros: Metro access, central to many attractions. Cons: the main tourist strip is busy and gritty in spots.

**Downtown Los Angeles** fits culture and food fans. Pros: museums, arenas, rail hub. Cons: some blocks feel empty at night.

**West Hollywood** appeals to nightlife seekers and shoppers. Pros: lively bars, restaurants and the Sunset Strip. Cons: limited rail access.

**Venice** is great for a laid-back, creative vibe. Pros: beach, Abbot Kinney Boulevard shops. Cons: far from inland attractions; parking can be tight.

**Pasadena** suits a quieter stay. Pros: historic Old Pasadena, gardens, Metro rail. Cons: a longer trip to the beaches.

## Things to know before you go

- Distances are large; group sightseeing by area to avoid spending hours in traffic.
- Tipping norms follow the rest of the US: roughly 15 to 20 percent at restaurants, plus tips for bartenders, valets and drivers.
- International visitors should check official sources for entry rules; domestic flyers should make sure their ID meets current TSA requirements.
- Check official government travel advisories before traveling. Do not leave valuables visible in parked cars, a common-sense precaution anywhere.
- Unofficial "celebrity homes" sellers and costumed characters on Hollywood Boulevard may ask for money; agree on any cost before engaging, and book tours through established operators.
- Wear sunscreen even on cool days, and carry water on hikes.

## Getting around

A car offers the most flexibility, though traffic and parking costs add up. The Metro rail and bus network connects Downtown, Hollywood, Pasadena, Santa Monica and Long Beach, and works well if you stay near a station. Ride-hailing is widely used. At LAX, a shuttle links terminals to the airport's transit connection and ride-share pickup area. Burbank, Long Beach and Ontario airports can be easier alternatives depending on where you stay.

## Best time to visit

Spring and fall offer warm, pleasant weather and fewer crowds. Early summer can bring coastal clouds known locally as "June Gloom." Late summer and early fall are hottest inland, and winter is mild with most of the year's rain.

## Budget tips

- Base yourself in one area and use transit for day trips to cut car-rental and parking costs.
- Many top attractions, including Griffith Observatory and the Getty Center, are free to enter (parking may cost extra).
- Taco trucks, food halls and strip-mall restaurants offer some of the city's best eating for less.
- Compare rates at airports beyond LAX for both flights and rental cars.
- Visit theme parks on weekdays outside school holidays, and look for multi-day or advance-purchase tickets.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a car in Los Angeles?

Not strictly. If you stay in a transit-friendly area such as Santa Monica, Hollywood or Downtown, you can combine Metro and ride-hailing. A car helps for beaches, canyons and day trips.

### How many days should I spend in Los Angeles?

Four to six days lets you see a mix of beaches, museums and neighborhoods without rushing.

### Is the ocean warm enough to swim?

The Pacific here is cool for much of the year. Late summer and early fall usually bring the most comfortable water temperatures.

### Can I see the Hollywood Sign up close?

Access to the sign itself is restricted. Griffith Observatory, Lake Hollywood Park and several hiking trails offer good viewpoints.
