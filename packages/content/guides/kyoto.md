## Why visit Kyoto

Kyoto served as Japan's imperial capital for over a thousand years, and it remains the heart of traditional Japanese culture. The city is home to many temples and shrines, serene gardens, wooden townhouses, and refined cuisine. You can walk through tunnels of vermilion torii gates, wander bamboo groves, and glimpse traditional arts in historic districts. Kyoto rewards slow travel, early mornings, and respect for its living traditions.

## Top sights and experiences

- **Fushimi Inari Taisha** — Thousands of torii gates winding up a forested mountain.
- **Kinkaku-ji** — The Golden Pavilion reflected in a pond.
- **Kiyomizu-dera** — A hillside temple with a famous wooden stage.
- **Arashiyama Bamboo Grove** — Towering bamboo stalks; go early.
- **Gion** — The historic geisha district of wooden teahouses.
- **Ginkaku-ji and the Philosopher's Path** — A temple and a canal-side walk.
- **Nishiki Market** — A narrow market street of local foods.
- **Ryōan-ji** — A famous Zen rock garden.
- **Nijō Castle** — A shogun's residence with "nightingale floors."
- **Day trip to Nara** — Ancient temples and freely roaming deer.

## Where to stay: best neighborhoods

**Downtown Kyoto** (around Kawaramachi and Karasuma) is central, with shopping, restaurants, and transport links. It suits first-timers.

**Gion** offers atmosphere and traditional streets. It's romantic but busy and generally pricey.

**Kyoto Station** area is practical for trains and airport access, with many hotels, though it feels more modern and less charming.

**Higashiyama** places you near temples and historic lanes. It suits culture lovers, with quieter evenings, but some areas are hilly.

**Arashiyama** is scenic and calm, known for ryokan stays and nature, though it's on the western edge of the city.

## Things to know before you go

- **Entry:** US citizens can generally visit Japan for short tourist stays without a visa. Check official sources before travel.
- **Airports:** Kyoto has no airport of its own. Most international travelers use Kansai International Airport near Osaka, connected by train.
- **Etiquette in Gion:** Do not chase, touch, or photograph geiko and maiko without permission. Some private lanes prohibit photography, and fines may apply. Follow posted signs.
- **Temple manners:** Speak quietly, follow photography rules, and remove shoes when required.
- **Tipping:** Not customary.
- **Luggage:** Buses are crowded, and large suitcases can be a burden. Consider forwarding luggage between hotels with a delivery service, a common practice in Japan.
- **Opening hours:** Many temples close in the late afternoon, so start your day early.
- **Cash:** Some temples and small restaurants are cash-only, so carry some yen.
- **Safety:** Kyoto is generally very safe. Check official government travel advisories before your trip.

## Getting around

City buses reach most sights but can be crowded in peak seasons. Subways and trains are often faster for longer distances. A rechargeable IC card works on most transport. Renting a bicycle is a great way to explore the flat central areas. Walking between nearby temples is pleasant. Kyoto Station connects to bullet trains for Tokyo and the airport express for Kansai.

## Best time to visit

Spring cherry blossoms and fall foliage are spectacular, but they bring heavy crowds and higher rates. Summer is hot and humid. Winter is cold yet peaceful, with fewer visitors and occasional snow on temples.

## Budget tips

- Visit top sights early in the morning to avoid crowds without paying for tours.
- Many shrines, including Fushimi Inari, are free to enter.
- Eat at casual noodle shops and convenience stores for affordable meals.
- Stay near Kyoto Station or downtown for value and convenience.
- Use a day pass if you'll ride buses and subways often; compare options first.
- Travel in winter or early summer for lower rates.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many days do I need in Kyoto?

Three days covers the highlights, with an extra day for Nara.

### Should I stay in a ryokan?

A ryokan stay, with tatami rooms and traditional meals, is a memorable cultural experience for at least one night.

### Can I visit Kyoto as a day trip from Osaka?

Yes, trains make it easy, but staying overnight lets you explore at quieter times.

### Is Kyoto crowded?

The most famous sites can be very busy. Visit early, explore lesser-known temples, and travel off-season.
