## Why visit Dubai

Dubai is a city of superlatives, home to record-breaking skyscrapers, enormous malls, and ambitious waterfront developments. Yet alongside the glitz you'll find traditional souks, wind-tower architecture, and wooden boats crossing Dubai Creek. Warm winter sunshine, long beaches, desert excursions, and a diverse international food scene make it a popular winter escape and a convenient stopover between continents, thanks to its major airline hub.

## Top sights and experiences

- **Burj Khalifa** — The world's tallest building, with observation decks.
- **Dubai Mall and Dubai Fountain** — Massive shopping and a choreographed fountain show.
- **Al Fahidi Historical Neighbourhood** — Restored wind-tower houses and small museums.
- **Abra ride across Dubai Creek** — A traditional boat crossing that costs very little.
- **Gold and Spice Souks** — Bustling traditional markets in Deira.
- **Museum of the Future** — A striking building with immersive exhibits.
- **Desert safari** — Dune drives, camel rides, and evening camps.
- **Jumeirah beaches** — Public beaches with views of the skyline.
- **Palm Jumeirah** — The famous palm-shaped artificial island.
- **Dubai Frame** — A giant picture frame with views of old and new Dubai.

## Where to stay: best neighborhoods

**Downtown Dubai** puts you near the Burj Khalifa, Dubai Mall, and fountain. It suits first-timers and shoppers, though it is pricey and busy.

**Dubai Marina** offers waterfront promenades, restaurants, and nightlife, with beach access nearby. It suits couples and groups but is far from Old Dubai.

**Jumeirah Beach Residence (JBR)** is ideal for beach lovers, with a lively beachfront walk. Expect crowds on weekends.

**Deira** is the historic trading district near the souks and creek. It offers good value and authentic atmosphere but is less polished.

**Business Bay** sits next to Downtown along the canal, often offering better rates with easy access to major sights.

**Al Barsha** is a convenient, value-oriented area near the Mall of the Emirates and Metro.

## Things to know before you go

- **Entry:** Rules for US citizens can change. Check official sources for current entry requirements and passport validity rules.
- **Laws and etiquette:** Dress modestly in public places such as malls and souks, covering shoulders and knees. Swimwear is fine at beaches and pools. Public displays of affection, public intoxication, and disrespectful behavior can lead to serious penalties.
- **Medications:** Some medications legal in the US are controlled in the UAE. Check official guidance before traveling with prescriptions.
- **Alcohol:** Available at licensed venues, typically hotels and some restaurants.
- **Ramadan:** During the holy month, be respectful by not eating, drinking, or smoking in public during daylight hours where requested. Some business hours change.
- **Tipping:** Around 10 percent in restaurants is common if no service charge is added.
- **Safety:** Dubai is generally very safe. Check official government travel advisories before your trip, and follow local laws closely.

## Getting around

The Dubai Metro is clean and efficient, with lines connecting the airport, Downtown, and the Marina. A rechargeable transit card covers the Metro, buses, trams, and water buses. Some Metro cars are reserved for women and children, and there is a premium class. Taxis and ride-hailing apps are widely available and affordable. Walking between sights can be difficult because of distances and heat.

## Best time to visit

November through March offers warm, pleasant weather and is peak season. Summer is extremely hot and humid, though hotel rates drop and indoor attractions keep visitors busy. Shoulder months are warm and less crowded.

## Budget tips

- Use the Metro and a transit card instead of taxis for longer routes.
- Take an abra across the creek for a cheap, memorable experience.
- Eat in Deira or Karama for flavorful, affordable meals.
- Visit free public beaches instead of paying for beach clubs.
- Book Burj Khalifa tickets online in advance, and consider non-peak times.
- Visit in summer for lower hotel rates, if you can handle the heat.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many days do I need in Dubai?

Three to four days covers the main sights, a desert trip, and beach time.

### What should I wear in Dubai?

Modest clothing in public places, with swimwear limited to beaches and pools.

### Can I drink alcohol in Dubai?

Yes, at licensed venues. Public intoxication is illegal.

### Is Dubai good for families?

Yes. It has theme parks, aquariums, beaches, and family-friendly malls.
