## Why visit Austin

Austin, the capital of Texas, calls itself the "Live Music Capital of the World," and its stages, food trucks and outdoor spaces back up the claim. The city blends a creative, independent spirit with a fast-growing tech scene. Visitors come for barbecue and breakfast tacos, nights of live music, swims in spring-fed pools and the chance to watch a huge bat colony fly out from under a downtown bridge on summer evenings.

## Top sights and experiences

- **Live music on Sixth Street and Red River** — dozens of venues spanning rock, country, blues and more.
- **Barton Springs Pool** — a spring-fed swimming hole in Zilker Park that stays cool year-round.
- **Texas State Capitol** — a grand, free-to-tour building that stands taller than the U.S. Capitol.
- **Congress Avenue Bridge bats** — a large urban bat colony emerges at dusk in warm months.
- **South Congress Avenue** — boutiques, vintage shops and murals with skyline views.
- **Lady Bird Lake** — paddleboarding, kayaking and a hike-and-bike trail around the water.
- **Barbecue** — Central Texas-style brisket draws lines at the most popular spots.
- **Bullock Texas State History Museum** — the story of Texas told through engaging exhibits.
- **Mount Bonnell** — a short climb for views of the Colorado River and the hills.
- **Texas Hill Country** — wineries, small towns and swimming holes within a short drive.

## Where to stay: best neighborhoods

**Downtown** suits first-timers and business travelers. Pros: walkable to music venues, the Capitol and the lake. Cons: busy and often expensive during big events.

**South Congress** works for shoppers and couples. Pros: eclectic shops, restaurants, skyline views. Cons: popular hotels book up fast.

**Rainey Street** fits nightlife seekers. Pros: converted bungalow bars, close to Downtown. Cons: loud on weekend nights.

**East Austin** appeals to food lovers and creative types. Pros: trendy restaurants, breweries, murals. Cons: hotels are fewer and more spread out.

**Zilker** is good for active travelers. Pros: near Barton Springs, trails and parks. Cons: mostly residential, with limited hotels.

## Things to know before you go

- Summer heat is intense; plan outdoor activities for mornings and evenings and drink plenty of water.
- Tipping follows US norms, roughly 15 to 20 percent at restaurants; tip counters at food trucks are common too.
- Domestic flyers should confirm their ID meets current TSA rules; international visitors should check official sources for entry requirements.
- Check official government travel advisories before your trip. Sixth Street gets crowded late at night, so stay with friends and keep belongings secure.
- Large festivals bring big crowds and higher hotel prices; check the event calendar before booking.
- Some popular swimming holes require reservations or close after heavy rain; check official park websites.

## Getting around

Downtown and South Congress are walkable. CapMetro runs buses and the MetroRail commuter line, and an airport bus connects to Downtown. Scooters, bike share and ride-hailing are widely used. A car is useful for Hill Country trips, but traffic on I-35 can be heavy and downtown parking costs add up.

## Best time to visit

Spring is popular for wildflowers and mild weather, while fall brings comfortable temperatures and festivals. Summer is very hot but great for swimming holes and the bat flights. Winter is generally mild and quieter.

## Budget tips

- Avoid major festival dates for better hotel rates.
- Enjoy free music at many bars and outdoor events.
- Eat at food trucks, which offer some of the city's best value meals.
- Swim at city pools and springs, which are often inexpensive.
- Stay farther from Downtown and use buses or ride-hailing.
- Get to barbecue spots early to avoid selling out and long waits.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many days do I need in Austin?

Three days covers live music, barbecue and outdoor highlights. Add a day or two for the Hill Country.

### When can I see the Austin bats?

The bats typically emerge at dusk from spring through fall, with the largest flights often in late summer.

### Is Austin walkable?

Downtown and nearby areas are walkable, but the city is spread out, so you will need transit or rides for other neighborhoods.

### Do I need a car in Austin?

Not for central Austin. A car is helpful for day trips into the Hill Country.
