## Why visit New Orleans

New Orleans feels unlike anywhere else in the United States. French, Spanish, African and Caribbean influences shaped its architecture, cuisine and music, and the city is widely credited as the birthplace of jazz. Brass bands, gumbo, beignets, balconies draped in ironwork and an easygoing approach to celebration make it an unforgettable trip for food lovers, music fans and history enthusiasts.

## Top sights and experiences

- **French Quarter** — the historic heart of the city, with Jackson Square and St. Louis Cathedral.
- **Frenchmen Street** — live jazz and brass bands in a string of clubs, a favorite with locals.
- **The National WWII Museum** — an extensive, immersive museum in the Warehouse District.
- **Garden District** — grand historic homes and oak-lined streets, best explored on foot.
- **St. Charles Avenue Streetcar** — a historic line rolling past mansions and Audubon Park.
- **Café du Monde and beignets** — powdered-sugar pastries and chicory coffee near the river.
- **Preservation Hall** — an intimate venue devoted to traditional New Orleans jazz.
- **Historic cemeteries** — above-ground tombs; some, like St. Louis Cemetery No. 1, require a licensed guide.
- **City Park** — sculpture garden, the New Orleans Museum of Art and huge oak trees.
- **Swamp tours** — wetlands and wildlife a short drive from the city.

## Where to stay: best neighborhoods

**French Quarter** suits first-timers who want to be in the middle of it all. Pros: walkable, historic, atmospheric. Cons: Bourbon Street area can be loud late at night.

**Central Business District** works for convenience and value. Pros: many hotels, near the Quarter and streetcars. Cons: less charm, busy during conventions.

**Warehouse District** fits museum lovers and foodies. Pros: galleries, WWII Museum, strong dining. Cons: quieter than the Quarter.

**Garden District** appeals to couples seeking a calmer, residential feel. Pros: beautiful streets, Magazine Street shops, streetcar access. Cons: a ride to the Quarter.

**Marigny** is good for music fans. Pros: steps from Frenchmen Street, colorful Creole cottages. Cons: mostly guesthouses and rentals.

## Things to know before you go

- Open containers are allowed in some parts of the city if drinks are in plastic cups, not glass; follow posted rules.
- Tipping is part of the culture, including for street musicians you enjoy.
- Domestic flyers should confirm their ID meets current TSA rules; international visitors should check official sources for entry requirements.
- Check official government travel advisories before your trip. Stick to busy, well-lit streets at night and use a ride-hail or taxi when returning late.
- A well-known street bet involves someone offering to guess "where you got your shoes"; politely decline and keep walking.
- Book cemetery and ghost tours with licensed, established operators.
- Mardi Gras and major festivals transform the city; expect crowds, street closures and higher prices.

## Getting around

The French Quarter, Marigny and CBD are walkable. Streetcars run on St. Charles Avenue, Canal Street and the riverfront, and buses fill the gaps. A ferry crosses the Mississippi to Algiers Point. Ride-hailing and taxis are easy to find. Parking in the Quarter is limited, so a car is only needed for swamp tours or plantation visits outside the city.

## Best time to visit

Late winter and spring offer pleasant weather and a packed festival calendar, including Mardi Gras season. Fall is also comfortable. Summer is hot and humid with afternoon storms, and hurricane season runs June through November, though summer can bring lower hotel rates.

## Budget tips

- Avoid peak festival periods if you want lower hotel rates.
- Enjoy free live music on Royal Street and in Jackson Square.
- Use streetcar passes instead of taxis for sightseeing.
- Look for happy hour deals, which are common across the city.
- Try po'boys, red beans and rice and neighborhood spots for affordable meals.
- Compare hotels in the CBD, which often cost less than the Quarter.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many days do I need in New Orleans?

Three to four days is ideal to explore the Quarter, Garden District, museums and music scene.

### Is New Orleans good for families?

Yes. The Audubon Zoo and Aquarium, City Park, streetcars and museums appeal to kids, though Bourbon Street at night is aimed at adults.

### When is Mardi Gras?

The date of Mardi Gras changes every year because it is tied to Easter. Parades happen over the weeks leading up to Fat Tuesday.

### Do I need a car in New Orleans?

No, not for the main neighborhoods. Walking, streetcars and ride-hailing work well.
