## Why visit Cartagena

Cartagena, on Colombia's Caribbean coast, is known for its colorful colonial architecture, flower-draped balconies and historic fortifications. Its Walled City is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and its lively plazas, rooftop bars and seafood restaurants make it a favorite for couples and culture lovers. Nearby islands offer white-sand beaches and clear water, making it easy to combine history and relaxation in one trip.

The city's food reflects its coastal setting and its African, Indigenous and Spanish roots, with fried fish, coconut rice, arepas and tropical fruit sold from carts on almost every corner. Music is never far away either, from champeta and cumbia to salsa spilling out of bars after dark. Cartagena is also a convenient starting point for exploring more of Colombia's Caribbean coast.

## Top sights and experiences

- **Walled City (Ciudad Amurallada)** — cobblestone streets, colonial mansions and plazas.
- **Castillo San Felipe de Barajas** — a massive Spanish fortress with tunnels and views.
- **Getsemaní** — street art, music and a lively evening scene.
- **City walls** — walk along the old fortifications at sunset.
- **Rosario Islands** — a coral archipelago for beaches and snorkeling.
- **Palace of the Inquisition** — a colonial building now housing a history museum.
- **Convento de la Popa** — a hilltop monastery with panoramic views.
- **Bazurto Market** — a bustling local market for food and produce.
- **Playa Blanca** — a popular beach on Barú, best visited on a quieter weekday.
- **Gold Museum** — free exhibits on pre-Columbian goldwork and Indigenous cultures.

## Where to stay: best neighborhoods

**Walled City** suits first-timers and couples. Pros: walkable, historic, boutique hotels. Cons: higher prices and busy streets.

**Getsemaní** works for younger travelers and nightlife fans. Pros: street art, bars, hostels and guesthouses. Cons: noisy at night.

**Bocagrande** fits families and beach lovers. Pros: high-rise hotels, beaches, modern amenities. Cons: less charm.

**San Diego** appeals to couples seeking quieter streets. Pros: part of the old city with a calmer atmosphere. Cons: limited budget options.

**Manga** is good for value seekers. Pros: residential, local feel. Cons: fewer tourist amenities.

## Things to know before you go

- US travelers need a valid passport; check official sources for current entry requirements and any pre-arrival forms.
- Check official government travel advisories before your trip, and stay alert to your surroundings, especially at night.
- Heat and humidity are intense; plan sightseeing for mornings and late afternoons.
- Tipping: restaurants often add a suggested service charge, which you can accept or decline.
- Beach and street vendors can be persistent; a polite "no, gracias" usually works.
- Agree on prices for boat trips, taxis and services in advance.
- Drink bottled water.
- Dress lightly but respectfully when visiting churches, and carry a hat and sunscreen.
- Street photographers and performers in the old town may expect a tip after posing or playing for you; decide before you engage.
- Some island trips include fees or taxes that are collected at the dock; ask what is included when you book.

## Getting around

The Walled City and Getsemaní are walkable. Taxis are common and inexpensive, but agree on a fare first, as many do not use meters. Ride-hailing apps operate in Cartagena. Boats to the Rosario Islands leave from the main tourist dock or nearby piers. Rafael Núñez International Airport is close to the city center, so transfers are usually short. Horse-drawn carriage rides through the old town are offered in the evenings; confirm the route and price before you climb aboard.

## Best time to visit

December to April is the dry season with sunny weather and peak crowds. May to November is rainier, but showers often pass quickly. Holidays and festivals bring higher prices.

## Budget tips

- Travel in shoulder months for better rates.
- Stay in Getsemaní or Manga for value.
- Eat set lunches (menú del día) at local restaurants.
- Book island trips directly with operators and confirm what is included.
- Explore free sights like plazas, street art and the walls.
- Carry small bills for taxis and street food.
- Visit the free plazas and walls at sunset instead of paying for a rooftop.
- Consider staying outside major holiday weeks, when rates rise sharply.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many days do I need in Cartagena?

Three to four days covers the Walled City, Getsemaní and an island trip.

### Is Cartagena good for families?

Yes, with planning for heat and crowds. Beaches and forts appeal to kids.

### Can I drink the tap water?

Many travelers prefer bottled water to be safe.

### When is the rainy season?

Roughly May to November, with October often among the wettest months.
