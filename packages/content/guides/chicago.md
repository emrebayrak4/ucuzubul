## Why visit Chicago

Chicago is a showcase of American architecture, set along the shore of Lake Michigan with beaches, parks and a long lakefront trail. It has world-class museums, a deep blues and jazz heritage, celebrated improv comedy and a food scene that goes far beyond deep-dish pizza. The city is easy to navigate thanks to its grid layout and elevated trains, and its neighborhoods each have a distinct character.

## Top sights and experiences

- **Chicago Architecture River Cruise** — one of the best ways to learn how the skyline took shape.
- **Millennium Park and Cloud Gate** — the mirrored sculpture nicknamed "The Bean" and outdoor concerts in summer.
- **Art Institute of Chicago** — a major museum with famous Impressionist and American works.
- **Navy Pier** — lakefront attractions, boat tours and a Ferris wheel.
- **Skydeck or 360 Chicago** — observation decks with glass ledges and wide views.
- **Museum Campus** — the Field Museum, Shedd Aquarium and Adler Planetarium side by side.
- **Lakefront Trail** — walk or bike along beaches and parks with skyline views.
- **Wrigley Field** — the historic ballpark in Wrigleyville.
- **Lincoln Park Zoo** — a free zoo in one of the city's largest parks.
- **Live blues, jazz and improv** — clubs and comedy theaters keep the city's traditions alive.

## Where to stay: best neighborhoods

**The Loop** suits first-timers and culture lovers. Pros: next to Millennium Park, museums and every L line. Cons: quieter at night than residential areas.

**River North** works for food and nightlife fans. Pros: dense restaurant and bar scene, central. Cons: can be noisy.

**Magnificent Mile** fits shoppers and families. Pros: department stores, lake access, many hotels. Cons: busy and commercial.

**West Loop** appeals to food-focused travelers. Pros: famous restaurant row, converted warehouses. Cons: fewer big hotels.

**Lincoln Park** is good for a residential, park-side stay. Pros: zoo, lakefront, boutiques. Cons: limited hotel options.

**Wicker Park** suits younger travelers. Pros: indie shops, music venues, Blue Line to O'Hare. Cons: farther from lakefront sights.

## Things to know before you go

- Winters are cold and windy, so dress in warm layers from November through March.
- Tipping follows US norms, roughly 15 to 20 percent at sit-down restaurants.
- Domestic flyers should confirm their ID meets current TSA rules; international visitors should check official sources for entry requirements.
- Check official government travel advisories before your trip. As in any big city, stay aware of your surroundings at night and on quiet train platforms.
- Street performers and people selling items near tourist areas may ask for money; you are under no obligation to pay.
- Locals are proud of their food, including deep-dish and tavern-style pizza, Italian beef and Chicago-style hot dogs.

## Getting around

The CTA's L trains and buses reach most places visitors want to go, and you can pay with a Ventra card, the Ventra app or a contactless bank card. The Blue Line runs directly to O'Hare and the Orange Line to Midway. Divvy bike share is useful in warmer months, and water taxis run along the river and lake. Driving downtown is slow, and parking is limited.

## Best time to visit

Late spring through early fall is the most popular time, with festivals, beaches and outdoor dining. September and October offer comfortable temperatures and fall color. Winter is cold, but holiday markets, museums and indoor dining keep things lively, and hotel rates are often lower.

## Budget tips

- Visit in winter or early spring for lower hotel prices.
- Many attractions are free, including Millennium Park, Lincoln Park Zoo and the Lakefront Trail.
- Check museum websites for free days, which are often aimed at Illinois residents.
- Use the L instead of taxis from the airports.
- Compare sightseeing passes if you plan to visit several paid attractions.
- Look for weekday rates, since convention weeks can drive up downtown prices.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many days do I need in Chicago?

Three to four days covers the main museums, architecture and a few neighborhoods.

### Which airport is better, O'Hare or Midway?

O'Hare has more routes and international flights. Midway is closer to downtown and served by several low-cost carriers. Both connect to the L.

### Is Chicago worth visiting in winter?

Yes, if you dress warmly. Museums, theater, dining and holiday events make it rewarding, and crowds are thinner.

### Do I need a car in Chicago?

No. Public transit, walking and ride-hailing cover most visitor needs.
