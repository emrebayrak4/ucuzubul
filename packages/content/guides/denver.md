## Why visit Denver

Denver sits at the edge of the Great Plains with the Rocky Mountains rising to the west, earning it the nickname "Mile High City" for its elevation of about 5,280 feet. It is a lively city in its own right, with craft breweries, a growing food scene, major sports teams and strong museums, and it is also the gateway to some of Colorado's best hiking, skiing and scenic drives. Many travelers combine a few city days with time in the mountains.

## Top sights and experiences

- **Red Rocks Park and Amphitheatre** — a dramatic outdoor concert venue set among giant sandstone formations.
- **Denver Art Museum** — wide-ranging collections in buildings known for bold architecture.
- **Union Station** — a restored rail hub with restaurants, bars and trains to the airport.
- **Larimer Square** — a historic block of shops and restaurants under strings of lights.
- **RiNo Art District** — murals, galleries, breweries and food halls.
- **Colorado State Capitol** — one step is marked at exactly one mile above sea level.
- **Denver Botanic Gardens** — themed gardens and seasonal events.
- **Rocky Mountain National Park** — alpine scenery and wildlife about two hours away; timed entry may apply.
- **Coors Field and the sports scene** — catch a baseball game in a lively downtown ballpark.
- **Mountain towns** — ski areas and historic towns along the I-70 corridor.

## Where to stay: best neighborhoods

**LoDo** suits first-timers and sports fans. Pros: walkable dining, bars and ballpark. Cons: lively weekend crowds.

**Union Station** works for travelers without a car. Pros: direct train to the airport, central location. Cons: hotel rates can be higher.

**RiNo** fits creative types and beer lovers. Pros: street art, breweries, trendy restaurants. Cons: still developing in places, with spread-out blocks.

**Capitol Hill** appeals to budget-conscious and nightlife-minded travelers. Pros: historic mansions, bars and music venues. Cons: fewer large hotels.

**Cherry Creek** is good for shoppers. Pros: upscale stores, galleries, quieter feel. Cons: a drive or bus ride to downtown.

**Highlands** suits foodies. Pros: neighborhood restaurants, views of downtown. Cons: mostly small hotels and rentals.

## Things to know before you go

- The altitude can cause shortness of breath, headaches or poor sleep. Drink extra water, go easy on alcohol at first, and take it slow.
- The sun is strong at elevation; use sunscreen even on cool days.
- Tipping follows US norms, roughly 15 to 20 percent at restaurants.
- Domestic flyers should confirm their ID meets current TSA rules; international visitors should check official sources for entry requirements.
- Check official government travel advisories before your trip, and use normal city awareness around downtown at night.
- Mountain weather changes quickly; carry layers and check road conditions before driving I-70 in winter.
- Recreational cannabis is legal under state law for adults, but consuming it in public places and on federal land is prohibited.

## Getting around

The A Line commuter train connects Denver International Airport with Union Station. Light rail and buses serve downtown and the suburbs, and a free shuttle runs along the 16th Street area. Downtown is walkable, and scooters and ride-hailing are common. You will want a car, shuttle or tour for mountain trips, and winter tires or chains may be required on some roads.

## Best time to visit

Summer is ideal for hiking, concerts at Red Rocks and outdoor dining. Fall brings golden aspens in the mountains. Winter draws skiers to nearby resorts, while the city itself often sees sunny days between snowfalls. Spring weather is changeable.

## Budget tips

- Take the train from the airport instead of a taxi.
- Visit in shoulder seasons for lower hotel rates.
- Enjoy free attractions like the Capitol, parks and many trails.
- Check museum free days, which often target Colorado residents.
- Compare ski passes and rentals in advance rather than buying at the window.
- Explore happy hours and food halls for affordable meals.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does it take to adjust to Denver's altitude?

Most people feel fine within a day or two. Stay hydrated and avoid heavy exertion on your first day.

### Can I ski from Denver without a car?

Yes, some resorts are reachable by shuttle services, buses or seasonal ski trains. Check schedules before you go.

### How many days do I need in Denver?

Two to three days covers the city. Add several days for Rocky Mountain National Park or ski towns.

### Is Red Rocks open when there are no concerts?

The park is generally open to visitors during the day, though access may be limited on show days.
