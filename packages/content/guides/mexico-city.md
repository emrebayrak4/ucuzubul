## Why visit Mexico City

Mexico City is one of the largest cities in the Americas and one of the most culturally rich. Built on the site of the Aztec capital Tenochtitlan, it layers pre-Hispanic ruins, colonial churches and modern architecture. The city's museums are outstanding, its tree-lined neighborhoods invite long walks, and its food, from street tacos to acclaimed restaurants, is a reason to visit on its own.

## Top sights and experiences

- **Zocalo and the Historic Center** — the vast main square, flanked by the Metropolitan Cathedral and National Palace.
- **Templo Mayor** — the excavated remains of the main Aztec temple, with an excellent museum.
- **National Museum of Anthropology** — one of the world's great museums of pre-Hispanic cultures.
- **Teotihuacan** — the Pyramids of the Sun and Moon, a day trip north of the city.
- **Frida Kahlo Museum (Casa Azul)** — the artist's former home in Coyoacan; tickets often sell out.
- **Chapultepec Park and Castle** — a huge urban park with museums and city views.
- **Palacio de Bellas Artes** — an Art Nouveau and Art Deco landmark with murals and performances.
- **Xochimilco** — colorful trajinera boats along ancient canals.
- **Street food** — tacos al pastor, tamales and quesadillas from stands and markets.
- **Lucha libre** — an energetic, family-friendly night of Mexican wrestling.

## Where to stay: best neighborhoods

**Roma Norte** suits food lovers and first-timers. Pros: cafes, restaurants, nightlife, leafy streets. Cons: some streets get noisy at night.

**Condesa** works for couples and walkers. Pros: Art Deco buildings, parks, dog-friendly vibe. Cons: popular and busy on weekends.

**Polanco** fits luxury and business travelers. Pros: upscale shopping, top restaurants, near museums. Cons: higher prices and a more polished, less local feel.

**Centro Historico** appeals to history and architecture fans. Pros: walk to major sights. Cons: quieter at night in some areas.

**Coyoacan** is good for a slower pace. Pros: colonial charm, markets, Frida Kahlo Museum. Cons: farther from other neighborhoods.

**Juarez** suits travelers who want central convenience. Pros: near Reforma, Roma and Chapultepec. Cons: mixed streetscapes.

## Things to know before you go

- The city sits at about 7,350 feet (2,240 meters); take it easy on your first day and drink water.
- US travelers need a valid passport; check official sources for current entry requirements.
- Check official government travel advisories before your trip, and use normal big-city awareness in crowded places like the Metro.
- Tipping around 10 to 15 percent is typical at sit-down restaurants.
- Drink bottled or filtered water.
- Use app-based ride-hailing or authorized taxi stands at the airport rather than accepting rides from people who approach you.
- Some ATMs and card terminals may offer to charge in US dollars; choosing pesos is usually better.
- A few words of Spanish and polite greetings go a long way.

## Getting around

The Metro is extensive and inexpensive but very crowded at rush hour. Metrobus lines run on dedicated lanes along major avenues. Ride-hailing apps are widely used and convenient. EcoBici bike share is available in central areas, and many neighborhoods are walkable. Traffic is heavy, so allow plenty of time.

## Best time to visit

The dry season from October to April brings sunny days and cool mornings. Spring is warmest. The rainy season, roughly June to September, usually brings afternoon showers but greener landscapes. Major holidays like Day of the Dead draw crowds.

## Budget tips

- Eat at markets and street stalls for affordable, excellent food.
- Many museums offer free or discounted entry on certain days; check official websites.
- Use the Metro and Metrobus for low-cost transport.
- Stay in Juarez or Centro for value compared with Polanco.
- Book Teotihuacan transport by public bus instead of private tours.
- Withdraw from bank ATMs inside branches for better security and fees.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many days do I need in Mexico City?

Four to five days lets you explore key neighborhoods, museums and a day trip to Teotihuacan.

### Is the altitude a problem?

Most travelers adjust within a day or two. Stay hydrated and limit alcohol at first.

### Is it easy to get around without Spanish?

In tourist areas, yes, but learning basic phrases helps and is appreciated.

### Can I drink the tap water?

It is generally recommended to drink bottled or filtered water.
