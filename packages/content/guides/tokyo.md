## Why visit Tokyo

Tokyo is a vast, endlessly fascinating metropolis where ancient temples sit among neon-lit skyscrapers. It is famously clean, efficient, and safe, with an unmatched food scene that ranges from tiny noodle counters to refined sushi bars. Each neighborhood feels like a city of its own, from the fashion streets of Harajuku to the traditional lanes of Asakusa. Whether you're interested in pop culture, gardens, shopping, or history, Tokyo rewards curiosity at every turn.

## Top sights and experiences

- **Sensō-ji** — Tokyo's oldest temple, in the historic Asakusa district.
- **Meiji Jingu** — A serene shrine set in a forest near Harajuku.
- **Shibuya Crossing** — The world-famous scramble intersection.
- **Shinjuku Gyoen** — A large garden, beautiful in cherry blossom season.
- **Tsukiji Outer Market** — Food stalls and shops for seafood and snacks.
- **Tokyo Skytree or Tokyo Tower** — Observation decks with city views.
- **Akihabara** — The hub for electronics, anime, and gaming.
- **teamLab digital art museums** — Immersive art experiences; book ahead.
- **Ueno Park and museums** — Including the Tokyo National Museum.
- **Day trip to Nikko, Kamakura, or Hakone** — Shrines, Buddhas, and hot springs.

## Where to stay: best neighborhoods

**Shinjuku** is a major transit hub with endless dining and nightlife. It suits first-timers, though the station is huge and busy.

**Shibuya** is youthful and energetic, great for shopping and nightlife, with excellent train connections.

**Ginza** is upscale and polished, with department stores and fine dining, and is central for sightseeing.

**Asakusa** offers traditional atmosphere and often better value, though it's farther from the west side's nightlife.

**Ueno** is convenient for airport access from Narita, with museums and a park, and is generally good value.

**Roppongi** is known for nightlife and art museums, with an international crowd.

## Things to know before you go

- **Entry:** US citizens can generally visit Japan for short tourist stays without a visa. Check official sources before travel and consider completing digital arrival forms in advance.
- **Etiquette:** Speak quietly on trains, avoid eating while walking in many settings, and remove shoes where indicated.
- **Tipping:** Not customary and can cause confusion. Excellent service is standard.
- **Luggage:** Rush-hour trains are crowded, and big suitcases are awkward. Hotel-to-hotel luggage delivery services are common and convenient.
- **Earthquake awareness:** Japan is well prepared for earthquakes. Note your hotel's emergency information and follow staff instructions if an alert sounds.
- **Cash:** Card acceptance is widespread, but some small restaurants and shrines are cash-only. Convenience-store ATMs typically accept foreign cards.
- **Trash:** Public bins are rare. Carry a small bag for your trash.
- **Safety:** Tokyo is generally very safe. In nightlife districts, be cautious of touts who invite you into bars, as unexpected charges can result. Check official government travel advisories before your trip.

## Getting around

Tokyo's trains and subways are punctual and extensive. A rechargeable IC card, or the mobile version, works on most trains, buses, and convenience stores. Navigation apps make transfers easy. Avoid rush hours if possible. Taxis are clean but pricier. From Narita and Haneda, express trains, monorail, and limousine buses connect to the city. Nationwide rail passes can be worth it for multi-city trips, but compare costs first.

## Best time to visit

Spring brings cherry blossoms and mild weather, and fall offers colorful foliage and comfortable temperatures. Summer is hot and humid with a rainy season early on. Winter is cold, dry, and often sunny, with fewer crowds.

## Budget tips

- Eat at convenience stores, ramen counters, and set-meal restaurants for great, inexpensive food.
- Use an IC card instead of buying individual tickets.
- Visit free attractions like shrines, temples, and parks.
- Check whether rail passes save you money versus individual tickets.
- Stay in business hotels or near outer stations for better value.
- Eat lunch sets, which are often cheaper than dinner.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many days do I need in Tokyo?

Four to five days covers the major neighborhoods. Add days for day trips.

### Is Tokyo hard to navigate without speaking Japanese?

Not usually. Train signs are in English, and navigation apps help.

### Should I buy a Japan Rail Pass?

It depends on your itinerary. Calculate individual fares and compare before buying.

### Which airport is closer to central Tokyo?

Haneda is closer to the city center than Narita, but both are well connected.
