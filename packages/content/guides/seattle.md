## Why visit Seattle

Seattle sits between Puget Sound and Lake Washington, with the Olympic and Cascade mountains on the horizon and Mount Rainier visible on clear days. The city is known for its coffee culture, seafood, music history and tech industry, but its biggest draw may be how quickly you can trade downtown streets for ferries, forests and alpine scenery. It is a great pick for travelers who like both city life and the outdoors.

## Top sights and experiences

- **Pike Place Market** — a historic public market with fishmongers, produce stalls and crafts.
- **Space Needle** — the city's iconic tower with a rotating glass floor and wide views.
- **Chihuly Garden and Glass** — vivid glass art installations next to the Space Needle.
- **Museum of Pop Culture** — music, film and pop-culture exhibits in a striking building.
- **Washington State Ferries** — a ride to Bainbridge Island delivers great skyline views.
- **Kerry Park** — a classic viewpoint of the skyline, with Mount Rainier on clear days.
- **Discovery Park** — the city's largest park, with bluffs, beaches and a lighthouse.
- **Pioneer Square and the Underground Tour** — the city's historic beginnings below street level.
- **Ballard Locks** — boats moving between lake and sound, plus a fish ladder in season.
- **Mount Rainier National Park** — a full-day trip to wildflower meadows and glaciers.

## Where to stay: best neighborhoods

**Downtown** suits first-timers. Pros: near Pike Place, waterfront and light rail. Cons: some blocks feel quiet or gritty after dark.

**Belltown** works for foodies and nightlife fans. Pros: bars, restaurants, walkable to the market. Cons: noise on weekends.

**South Lake Union** fits business travelers. Pros: modern hotels, lake views, streetcar. Cons: corporate feel.

**Capitol Hill** appeals to younger travelers. Pros: lively nightlife, LGBTQ+ friendly scene, light rail station. Cons: fewer large hotels.

**Queen Anne** is good for a residential, scenic stay. Pros: near Seattle Center, viewpoints. Cons: hilly, with limited transit.

**Pioneer Square** suits sports fans and history buffs. Pros: stadiums, historic architecture. Cons: some areas feel rough.

## Things to know before you go

- Bring a rain jacket in cooler months; locals often skip umbrellas in light drizzle.
- Tipping follows US norms, roughly 15 to 20 percent at restaurants.
- International visitors should check official sources for entry requirements; domestic flyers should confirm their ID meets TSA rules.
- Check official government travel advisories before your trip. Visible homelessness exists in some central areas; use normal city awareness and never leave items in parked cars.
- National park entry and some popular trails may require timed reservations in summer; check the National Park Service website.
- Wildfire smoke can affect air quality in late summer.

## Getting around

The Link light rail runs from Sea-Tac Airport through downtown and up to the University District and beyond. Buses, streetcars and the monorail between downtown and Seattle Center cover much of the rest. Pay with an ORCA card or app. Ferries connect to Bainbridge Island and other destinations. A car is helpful for day trips but not needed downtown.

## Best time to visit

Summer offers dry, sunny weather and long days, making it peak season. Late spring and early fall are pleasant and less crowded. Winter is mild but gray and wet, with lower hotel prices. Cruise season brings extra visitors from spring through fall.

## Budget tips

- Visit outside summer for lower hotel rates.
- Take light rail from the airport rather than a taxi.
- Ride the ferry to Bainbridge Island as an affordable scenic outing.
- Compare city attraction passes if you plan to visit several paid sights.
- Enjoy free parks, viewpoints and the waterfront.
- Eat at market stalls, teriyaki shops and food trucks.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does it rain all the time in Seattle?

No. Rain is frequent in fall and winter, often as drizzle, while summers are usually dry and sunny.

### How many days do I need in Seattle?

Three days covers the main sights. Add days for Mount Rainier, the Olympic Peninsula or the San Juan Islands.

### Is Seattle a good base for cruises to Alaska?

Yes, Seattle is a major departure port for Alaska cruises in season.

### Do I need a car in Seattle?

Not for the city. A car or tour helps for national parks and other day trips.
