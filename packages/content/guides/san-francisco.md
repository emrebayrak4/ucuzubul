## Why visit San Francisco

Compact, hilly and surrounded by water on three sides, San Francisco is one of America's most scenic cities. Victorian houses, cable cars, foggy bay views and distinct neighborhoods give it a strong sense of place. The food scene draws on nearby farms and a long immigrant history, and wine country, redwood forests and the Pacific coast are all within day-trip range.

## Top sights and experiences

- **Golden Gate Bridge** — walk or bike across for views of the bay and city skyline.
- **Alcatraz Island** — the former federal prison, reached by ferry; book well ahead.
- **Cable cars** — a moving historic landmark climbing the city's steep hills.
- **Golden Gate Park** — museums, gardens and meadows stretching toward the ocean.
- **Chinatown** — one of the oldest Chinatowns in North America, full of shops and bakeries.
- **Ferry Building Marketplace** — local food vendors and a busy farmers market on certain days.
- **Mission District** — colorful murals, Mission Dolores and famous burritos.
- **Lands End Trail** — coastal paths with views of the Golden Gate and old shipwreck sites.
- **San Francisco Museum of Modern Art** — a major collection of modern and contemporary art.
- **Muir Woods** — towering coast redwoods just north of the bridge; reservations are required for parking or shuttle.

## Where to stay: best neighborhoods

**Union Square** suits first-timers and shoppers. Pros: central, many hotels, transit hub. Cons: some nearby blocks, including parts of the Tenderloin, can feel rough.

**Fisherman's Wharf** works for families. Pros: waterfront, close to Pier 39 and Alcatraz ferries. Cons: touristy, with fewer local dining options.

**Nob Hill** fits couples seeking classic elegance. Pros: views, historic hotels, cable car lines. Cons: steep hills.

**SoMa** appeals to business and culture travelers. Pros: museums, convention center, Caltrain and BART access. Cons: can feel corporate and quiet at night.

**Mission District** is great for food lovers and younger travelers. Pros: lively, sunnier microclimate, nightlife. Cons: fewer traditional hotels.

**Marina District** suits active travelers. Pros: waterfront paths, near the Presidio and bridge. Cons: farther from downtown transit.

## Things to know before you go

- Pack layers. Fog and wind can make summer days chilly, and temperatures shift between neighborhoods.
- Tipping follows US norms, and some restaurants add a surcharge for employee benefits; read the bill.
- International visitors should check official sources for US entry rules; domestic flyers should verify their ID meets TSA requirements.
- Check official government travel advisories before your trip. Car break-ins are a known problem, so never leave anything visible in a parked car.
- Book Alcatraz tickets only through the official concessioner to avoid resellers charging extra.
- Visible homelessness is common in some central areas; be respectful and use normal urban awareness.

## Getting around

San Francisco is very walkable if you accept the hills. Muni buses, light rail, historic streetcars and cable cars cover the city, and you can pay with the Clipper card or app or a contactless card. BART connects the city to SFO, Oakland and the East Bay. Ride-hailing is plentiful. A car is a burden in the city because of parking and break-ins, but useful for day trips.

## Best time to visit

September and October often bring the warmest, clearest weather of the year. Spring is pleasant and green. Summer draws the most visitors but can be cool and foggy, while winter is mild with most of the rain.

## Budget tips

- Stay near BART or Muni and skip a rental car until you need one for day trips.
- Walk across the Golden Gate Bridge, explore parks and visit free viewpoints like Twin Peaks.
- Many museums offer free days for residents or the public on a set schedule; check websites.
- Eat in the Mission, Chinatown and Richmond District for good value.
- Visit Sunday-to-Thursday, when hotel rates are often lower outside major conventions.
- Consider a transit pass if you plan several cable car rides.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many days do I need in San Francisco?

Three days covers the highlights. Add a day or two for Muir Woods, Napa or Sonoma, or the coast.

### Is it cold in San Francisco in summer?

It can be. Fog often keeps summer temperatures cool, especially near the ocean, so bring a jacket.

### Should I fly into SFO or Oakland?

SFO has more flights and a direct BART link. Oakland can be cheaper and is also on BART.

### Do I need to reserve Alcatraz in advance?

Yes, tours often sell out, especially in summer and on weekends.
