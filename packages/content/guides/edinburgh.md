## Why visit Edinburgh

Scotland's capital is one of the most dramatic cities in Europe, with a castle perched on a volcanic rock, a medieval Old Town of narrow closes, and an elegant Georgian New Town, together recognized as a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Wild green spaces sit right in the city, including an extinct volcano you can climb. Edinburgh is famous for its summer festivals, cozy pubs, and whisky culture, and it is a gateway to the Scottish Highlands.

## Top sights and experiences

- **Edinburgh Castle** — A fortress with centuries of history and sweeping views.
- **Royal Mile** — The historic spine of the Old Town, from castle to palace.
- **Palace of Holyroodhouse** — The monarch's official residence in Scotland.
- **Arthur's Seat** — A rewarding hike to the top of an ancient volcano.
- **National Museum of Scotland** — Wide-ranging collections, free to enter, with a rooftop terrace.
- **Calton Hill** — Monuments and classic city views, especially at sunset.
- **Princes Street Gardens** — Green space beneath the castle.
- **Dean Village** — A picturesque riverside hamlet within the city.
- **Royal Yacht Britannia** — The former royal yacht, moored in Leith.
- **Whisky tasting** — Learn about Scotland's national drink.

## Where to stay: best neighborhoods

**Old Town** is atmospheric and central to the main sights. It suits first-timers, though streets are steep and crowded in summer.

**New Town** offers Georgian grandeur, shopping, and dining with easy access to the train station. It's elegant and convenient, with higher rates.

**Stockbridge** feels like a village, with independent shops, cafes, and a Sunday market. It suits those seeking calm, a short walk from the center.

**Leith**, the historic port, has a strong food and bar scene along the waterfront, connected by tram. It offers good value and local character.

**West End** is close to the center and theaters, with stylish restaurants and a quieter vibe.

## Things to know before you go

- **Entry:** US citizens currently need an Electronic Travel Authorisation (ETA) to visit the UK. Check official sources before booking.
- **Visitor levy:** Edinburgh has introduced a visitor levy on overnight stays, typically collected by accommodations.
- **Weather:** Conditions change quickly. Pack layers and waterproofs, even in summer.
- **Tipping:** Around 10 percent for table service is common if no service charge is added.
- **Driving:** Cars drive on the left. If you rent a car for the Highlands, collect it on your way out of the city, as central parking is limited.
- **Closes and steps:** The Old Town's narrow alleys, called closes, are worth exploring, but many involve steep stairs.
- **Festival season:** August brings the city's major festivals; book accommodation very early, as rates and demand rise sharply.
- **Safety:** Edinburgh is generally safe. Use normal precautions against pickpockets in crowded areas, especially during festivals. On Arthur's Seat, wear sturdy shoes and avoid steep paths in wet or windy weather. Check official government travel advisories before your trip.

## Getting around

The city center is compact and walkable, though hilly. Lothian buses and the tram cover the city; contactless payment is widely accepted. The tram connects the airport with the city center and Leith. Waverley Station has trains to Glasgow, London, and the Highlands.

## Best time to visit

Late spring through early fall offers longer days and milder weather. August is the busiest month. Winter is cold and dark, but the city's festive season and New Year celebrations are popular.

## Budget tips

- Enjoy free museums, like the National Museum of Scotland and the national galleries.
- Hike Arthur's Seat and Calton Hill for free views.
- Visit outside August for much better accommodation rates.
- Use day tickets on buses and trams if you'll ride often.
- Book castle tickets online in advance.
- Look for lunch specials at pubs and restaurants.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many days do I need in Edinburgh?

Two to three days covers the main sights. Add days for a Highlands tour.

### Is Edinburgh walkable?

Yes, but it's hilly and has many steps. Comfortable shoes are essential.

### When are the Edinburgh festivals?

The main festivals take place in August. Check official festival websites for exact dates each year.

### Can I visit the Highlands from Edinburgh?

Yes. Many day tours depart from the city, and trains and rental cars make longer trips easy.
