## Why visit Paris

Paris earns its reputation. Grand boulevards, riverside quays, and neighborhood cafes make simply walking the city a pleasure, and its museums hold some of the most famous art in the world. Food is a daily highlight, from a morning croissant to a long dinner at a bistro. The city is compact enough to explore on foot, with an excellent Metro filling in the gaps, and it makes a natural base for day trips to Versailles, Giverny, or the Champagne region.

## Top sights and experiences

- **Eiffel Tower** — Ride to the top or simply admire it from the Trocadéro or Champ de Mars.
- **The Louvre** — A former royal palace holding art from antiquity to the 1800s.
- **Musée d'Orsay** — Impressionist masterpieces in a converted railway station.
- **Notre-Dame Cathedral** — The restored Gothic landmark on the Île de la Cité.
- **Sainte-Chapelle** — Soaring medieval stained glass, best on a sunny day.
- **Montmartre and Sacré-Cœur** — Hilltop village charm and sweeping city views.
- **Seine river cruise** — An easy way to see the major monuments, especially at dusk.
- **Le Marais** — Medieval lanes, mansions, boutiques, and the Place des Vosges.
- **Palace of Versailles** — The opulent royal residence and gardens, a short train ride away.
- **Luxembourg Gardens** — A classic Parisian park for people-watching.

## Where to stay: best neighborhoods

**Le Marais** (3rd and 4th arrondissements) is central, lively, and full of shops, galleries, and restaurants. It suits couples and first-timers, though rooms tend to be small and in demand.

**Saint-Germain-des-Prés** (6th) offers a classic Left Bank feel with cafes, bookshops, and easy access to the Louvre and Orsay. It is elegant and walkable but usually among the pricier areas.

**Latin Quarter** (5th) is historic and student-friendly, with more moderate options and lively streets. Some blocks near the main tourist lanes can feel busy and touristy.

**Montmartre** (18th) has village character and views, often with better value. It is hilly and farther from central sights, and the area around the base of the hill can be hectic at night.

**Opéra and Grands Boulevards** (9th) are convenient for transit, department stores, and airport connections, making them practical for short stays and shoppers.

## Things to know before you go

- **Entry:** France is in the Schengen Area. US citizens can generally visit for short stays without a visa, but the EU is rolling out new digital border and travel authorization systems. Check official sources before you travel.
- **Greetings matter:** Say "bonjour" when you enter a shop or restaurant and "au revoir" when you leave. It sets a friendly tone.
- **Tipping:** Service is included in restaurant prices. Rounding up or leaving a few coins for good service is appreciated but not required.
- **Dining hours:** Many restaurants serve lunch and dinner at set times, with kitchens closed in the mid-afternoon. Cafes and brasseries fill the gaps.
- **Safety:** Paris is generally safe, but pickpocketing is common on the Metro and at major sights. Be cautious of people asking you to sign petitions, offering friendship bracelets, or claiming to have found a ring. Check official government travel advisories before your trip.
- **Closures:** Many museums close one day a week, so verify opening days before planning.

## Getting around

The Metro and RER commuter trains reach almost everywhere quickly. Contactless and mobile ticketing options make paying easier than in the past; check the transit authority's app for current choices. Buses are scenic, and the city's bike-share network and growing bike lanes make cycling practical. Walking along the Seine is often the best way to connect sights. From Charles de Gaulle and Orly, trains and buses are usually cheaper than taxis, though taxis to central Paris have regulated fares.

## Best time to visit

Spring and early fall offer pleasant temperatures and blooming or golden parks. Summer is busy and can be hot, and some smaller businesses close for part of August while locals vacation. Winter is cooler and grayer but brings shorter lines and festive decorations in December.

## Budget tips

- Many national museums offer free entry on certain days or for visitors under a certain age; check eligibility on official sites.
- Reserve timed tickets online for the Louvre, Eiffel Tower, and Versailles to avoid long lines.
- Order the fixed-price lunch menu at bistros for better value than dinner.
- Picnic with bread, cheese, and fruit from a market or bakery in a park.
- Drink at the counter in cafes, where prices are often lower than at a table.
- Consider staying in outer arrondissements near a direct Metro line.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many days should I spend in Paris?

Four to five days lets you see major museums and monuments and still enjoy slower cafe time. Add a day for Versailles.

### Do people in Paris speak English?

Many people working in tourism do, but starting with a polite "bonjour" and a few French phrases goes a long way.

### Is a museum pass worth it?

If you plan to visit several paid museums and monuments in a short time, a pass can save money and simplify entry. Compare it with the sights on your list.

### Is tap water safe to drink?

Yes. Paris tap water is safe, and you can ask for a free carafe of water ("une carafe d'eau") at restaurants.
