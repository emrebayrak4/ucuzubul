## Why visit Miami

Miami blends tropical beaches, Art Deco architecture and a strong Latin American and Caribbean influence that shapes its food, music and everyday life. You can spend the morning on the sand, the afternoon among the murals of Wynwood and the evening over Cuban coffee and late dinners. With the Everglades and the Florida Keys within reach, it also makes an excellent base for nature trips.

## Top sights and experiences

- **South Beach** — wide sand, turquoise water and a famous people-watching scene along Ocean Drive.
- **Art Deco Historic District** — pastel buildings from the 1930s and 1940s, best seen on a walking tour.
- **Wynwood Walls and Wynwood** — large-scale murals, galleries and breweries.
- **Little Havana** — Calle Ocho's cafes, cigar shops and Domino Park reflect Cuban heritage.
- **Vizcaya Museum and Gardens** — an early 20th-century estate with formal gardens on Biscayne Bay.
- **Pérez Art Museum Miami** — modern and contemporary art in a waterfront building.
- **Everglades National Park** — airboat rides, boardwalks and wildlife within an hour or so.
- **Key Biscayne** — calmer beaches, bike paths and a lighthouse at Bill Baggs Cape Florida State Park.
- **Design District** — high-end shops, architecture and public art.
- **Day trip to the Florida Keys** — the Overseas Highway is a memorable drive.

## Where to stay: best neighborhoods

**South Beach** suits first-timers and nightlife lovers. Pros: walkable to the beach, restaurants and Art Deco sights. Cons: busy, noisy and pricey in high season.

**Mid-Beach** works for a calmer beach stay. Pros: large resorts and quieter sand. Cons: fewer walkable dining options.

**Brickell** fits urban travelers and business visitors. Pros: high-rise hotels, restaurants, Metromover access. Cons: no beach on your doorstep.

**Downtown Miami** is convenient for museums, the cruise port and arenas. Pros: transit links, bayfront parks. Cons: some streets are quiet after office hours.

**Coconut Grove** appeals to couples and families. Pros: leafy, relaxed, waterfront parks. Cons: a drive to the beaches.

**Coral Gables** is good for a refined, residential stay. Pros: Mediterranean Revival architecture, the Venetian Pool. Cons: car-dependent.

## Things to know before you go

- Spanish is widely spoken, and you will hear it often, though English works everywhere.
- Some restaurants, especially in tourist areas, automatically add a service charge to the bill; check before adding a tip.
- International visitors should check official sources for entry rules; domestic flyers should confirm their ID meets current TSA requirements.
- Check official government travel advisories before your trip. Watch your belongings on crowded beaches and keep valuables out of sight in parked cars.
- On busy strips, some restaurants use street hosts to pull in diners; review the menu prices and drink sizes before ordering.
- Respect beach warning flags and lifeguard instructions; rip currents can occur.
- Sun and humidity are strong year-round, so pace yourself.

## Getting around

Miami is car-friendly, but traffic and parking can be frustrating, especially on Miami Beach. The free Metromover loops through Downtown and Brickell, Metrorail connects to the airport and Coral Gables area, and local trolleys serve several neighborhoods. Ride-hailing is common. Brightline trains link Miami with Fort Lauderdale, West Palm Beach and Orlando. Rent a car for the Everglades and the Keys.

## Best time to visit

Winter and spring bring warm, drier weather and the busiest season. Summer is hot, humid and stormy but quieter and better value. Hurricane season runs from June through November, so consider flexible bookings and travel insurance in those months.

## Budget tips

- Visit in late spring, summer or early fall for lower hotel rates, keeping hurricane season in mind.
- Stay in Downtown, Brickell or mainland neighborhoods and visit the beach by bus or ride-hail.
- Explore free attractions like Wynwood's street art, Little Havana and public beaches.
- Eat at Cuban cafes and ventanitas (walk-up windows) for affordable meals and coffee.
- Use the free Metromover and local trolleys.
- Check resort fees and parking charges, which can add significantly to Miami Beach stays.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is South Beach the best place to stay in Miami?

It is ideal if you want walkable beach and nightlife. Families or travelers seeking quiet may prefer Mid-Beach, Key Biscayne or Coconut Grove.

### How many days do I need in Miami?

Three to four days covers the beach, main neighborhoods and a museum or two. Add days for the Everglades or the Keys.

### Do I need a car in Miami?

Not if you stay in South Beach or Brickell and stick to central areas. A car is helpful for day trips.

### When is hurricane season in Miami?

The Atlantic hurricane season officially runs from June 1 to November 30. Monitor forecasts if you travel during those months.
