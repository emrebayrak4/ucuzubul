## Why visit London

London packs two thousand years of history, world-class museums, royal ceremony, and one of the most diverse food scenes on the planet into a city that is surprisingly easy to navigate. Many of its biggest national museums are free to enter, its parks are enormous, and every neighborhood feels like a different town, from the Georgian squares of Bloomsbury to the street art and markets of the East End. For US travelers there is no language barrier, and direct flights connect London to most major American airports.

## Top sights and experiences

- **Tower of London** — Nearly a thousand years of history, plus the Crown Jewels.
- **British Museum** — A vast collection spanning world civilizations, free to enter.
- **Westminster Abbey and the Houses of Parliament** — The heart of British church and state, with Big Ben's tower alongside.
- **South Bank walk** — A riverside stroll past theaters, galleries, and the London Eye.
- **Tate Modern** — Modern and contemporary art inside a former power station.
- **Borough Market** — One of the city's oldest food markets, best visited hungry.
- **Buckingham Palace and the Changing of the Guard** — Classic royal pageantry; check the schedule before you go.
- **West End theater** — Musicals and plays that rival Broadway.
- **Hyde Park and Kensington Gardens** — Green space for a picnic or a rowboat on the Serpentine.
- **Natural History Museum** — Dinosaur skeletons and striking architecture, popular with families.

## Where to stay: best neighborhoods

**Covent Garden** suits first-timers and theater lovers. You can walk to the West End, Trafalgar Square, and the river. The tradeoff is crowds and generally higher room rates.

**South Bank** puts you along the Thames near the London Eye, Tate Modern, and Borough Market. Views and walkability are excellent, though evenings are quieter away from the river.

**Kensington** is polished, leafy, and close to the big museums and Hyde Park. It works well for families and those who want calm, but it is a Tube ride from the nightlife.

**Shoreditch** appeals to younger travelers and food and bar fans, with markets, galleries, and late nights. It can be noisy on weekends and is farther from Westminster sights.

**Bloomsbury** offers a central, literary feel near the British Museum and good transit links, often at somewhat better value than Covent Garden.

**Paddington** is practical if you arrive on the Heathrow Express, with a wide range of hotel types, though the area itself has fewer attractions.

## Things to know before you go

- **Entry:** US citizens currently need an Electronic Travel Authorisation (ETA) to visit the UK for short stays. Rules change, so check official sources before you book.
- **Payment:** Contactless cards and phone wallets are accepted almost everywhere, including on public transit.
- **Tipping:** Many restaurants add a discretionary service charge; if not, leaving around 10 to 12.5 percent is common for table service. Tipping in pubs when ordering at the bar is not expected.
- **Etiquette:** Stand on the right on escalators, queue patiently, and keep voices down on the Tube.
- **Safety:** London is generally safe for visitors, but pickpocketing happens in crowded areas. Keep phones secured near busy stations and street crossings. Be wary of street games and petitions designed to distract. Check official government travel advisories before your trip.

## Getting around

The Underground (the Tube), buses, Overground, and Elizabeth line cover nearly everywhere. Tap the same contactless card or phone on every journey and fares are capped daily and weekly automatically. Walking is often faster than the Tube for short central hops. From Heathrow, the Elizabeth line and the Piccadilly line are budget-friendly alternatives to the Heathrow Express; Gatwick, Stansted, and Luton have their own rail links.

## Best time to visit

Late spring through early fall brings the longest days and the best odds of mild weather, along with the biggest crowds. Summer can be busy at major sights. Autumn and early spring offer fewer visitors and lower hotel rates, while December brings festive lights and markets. Rain is possible year-round, so pack layers and an umbrella.

## Budget tips

- Prioritize the free national museums and galleries.
- Use contactless payment for automatic fare capping rather than buying single tickets.
- Book trains and popular attractions in advance, when lower fares are often available.
- Look for same-day or rush theater tickets instead of paying full price.
- Eat a bigger meal at lunch, when many restaurants offer set menus, and use markets and supermarket meal deals for quick bites.
- Staying slightly outside Zone 1 near a good Tube line can save noticeably on hotels.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many days do I need in London?

Four to five days covers the major sights at a comfortable pace. A week lets you add day trips to places like Windsor, Oxford, or Bath.

### Is the London Pass or a sightseeing card worth it?

It depends on how many paid attractions you plan to see. Since many museums are free, compare the pass price against the attractions you will actually visit.

### Do I need cash in London?

Very little. Cards are accepted almost everywhere, and some places are card-only. A small amount of cash can be handy for some market stalls.

### Which airport should I fly into?

Heathrow has the most transatlantic flights and the easiest transit into central London, but Gatwick and other airports can offer competitive fares. Factor in transfer time and cost when comparing.
