## Why visit Vancouver

Vancouver is a city where mountains meet the ocean. Glass towers rise beside forested parks, sandy beaches and a busy harbor, with the Coast Mountains as a backdrop. It is a city for outdoor lovers, with kayaking, cycling and hiking minutes from downtown and ski areas close by. Add a diverse food scene, especially Asian cuisine and seafood, plus Indigenous art and culture, and it is one of North America's most appealing city breaks.

## Top sights and experiences

- **Stanley Park** — a vast urban park with the Seawall path, beaches and totem poles.
- **Granville Island** — a public market, artisan studios and waterfront cafes.
- **Capilano Suspension Bridge Park** — a high swinging bridge and treetop walkways in North Vancouver.
- **Grouse Mountain** — gondola rides to city views, hiking in summer and skiing in winter.
- **Gastown** — the city's historic quarter, home to the famous steam clock.
- **Museum of Anthropology at UBC** — renowned for First Nations art and Northwest Coast carvings.
- **English Bay and Kitsilano Beach** — sunsets, beach volleyball and seaside walks.
- **Chinatown** — one of the largest historic Chinatowns in Canada, with a classical Chinese garden.
- **Sea-to-Sky Highway** — a spectacular drive toward Squamish and Whistler.
- **Lynn Canyon Park** — forest trails and a free suspension bridge.

## Where to stay: best neighborhoods

**Downtown** suits first-timers. Pros: central, near shopping and transit. Cons: busy, with higher rates.

**Coal Harbour** works for luxury travelers. Pros: waterfront views, close to Stanley Park and the cruise terminal. Cons: pricey.

**West End** fits travelers who want a residential feel. Pros: near English Bay and Stanley Park, plenty of restaurants. Cons: fewer big hotels.

**Yaletown** appeals to food and nightlife fans. Pros: converted warehouses, patios, SkyTrain access. Cons: lively on weekends.

**Gastown** is good for history and style seekers. Pros: cobblestone streets, bars, boutiques. Cons: it borders the Downtown Eastside, where visible poverty and addiction are concentrated.

**Kitsilano** suits beach lovers. Pros: laid-back vibe, beaches, cafes. Cons: a bus ride to downtown.

## Things to know before you go

- US citizens flying to Canada need a valid passport, and requirements differ for land crossings. Check official sources before you go.
- Tipping is similar to the US, around 15 to 20 percent at restaurants.
- Check official government travel advisories before your trip, and avoid leaving anything visible in parked cars.
- Rain is common in fall and winter; pack waterproof layers.
- Respect wildlife on trails, including bears in mountain areas, and follow posted guidance.
- Many attractions and sites acknowledge the traditional territories of local First Nations; take time to learn about this history.
- Ride-share and taxi options are available, but always use licensed services.

## Getting around

SkyTrain's Canada Line connects Vancouver International Airport with downtown, and other lines serve the suburbs. Buses and the SeaBus ferry to North Vancouver round out the network; pay with a Compass card or a contactless card. Small ferries shuttle across False Creek to Granville Island. Biking is popular, with dedicated lanes and Mobi bike share. A car is mainly needed for trips outside the city.

## Best time to visit

Summer offers the driest, sunniest weather and is peak season. Spring brings cherry blossoms, and early fall is pleasant. Winter is mild and rainy in the city, while nearby mountains offer skiing and snowboarding.

## Budget tips

- Visit outside summer for lower hotel rates.
- Take SkyTrain from the airport.
- Enjoy free sights like Stanley Park, beaches and the Lynn Canyon suspension bridge.
- Use transit passes for full days of exploring.
- Eat at food courts, markets and Asian restaurants in Richmond for value.
- Look for packages that combine attractions like Grouse Mountain and Capilano.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many days do I need in Vancouver?

Three to four days covers the city's highlights. Add time for Whistler or Vancouver Island.

### Is Vancouver rainy?

Fall and winter are rainy, but summers tend to be dry and sunny.

### Can I visit Whistler as a day trip?

Yes, many visitors take a day trip by car or bus along the Sea-to-Sky Highway, though an overnight stay allows more time.

### Do I need a car in Vancouver?

No. Transit, walking and biking cover most of the city.
