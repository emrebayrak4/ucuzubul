## Why visit Hong Kong

Hong Kong packs a dramatic harbor skyline, steep green mountains, and centuries of Cantonese culture into a compact territory. You can ride a historic ferry across Victoria Harbour, eat dim sum in a bustling teahouse, and hike a ridge trail with sea views, all in the same day. Its neighborhoods range from glittering financial districts to traditional markets and temples. Excellent public transit and a dense urban layout make it easy to see a lot in a short visit.

## Top sights and experiences

- **Victoria Peak** — Panoramic views of the skyline, reached by the historic Peak Tram.
- **Star Ferry** — An inexpensive, iconic harbor crossing.
- **Tsim Sha Tsui Promenade** — Harbor views and the evening light show.
- **Tian Tan Buddha and Ngong Ping** — A giant bronze Buddha reached by cable car on Lantau Island.
- **Man Mo Temple** — An atmospheric temple in Sheung Wan.
- **Temple Street Night Market** — Street food and stalls in Kowloon.
- **M+ museum** — A major museum of visual culture in the West Kowloon Cultural District.
- **Dragon's Back trail** — A popular ridge hike with coastal views.
- **Dim sum** — A Cantonese tradition best enjoyed with tea.
- **Outlying islands** — Lamma or Cheung Chau for seafood and a slower pace.

## Where to stay: best neighborhoods

**Central** is the business hub with fine dining and nightlife around Lan Kwai Fong. It's convenient but pricey.

**Tsim Sha Tsui** offers harbor views, shopping, and museums on the Kowloon side, with plenty of hotel options.

**Wan Chai** mixes local markets, restaurants, and nightlife, with good transit and often better value.

**Causeway Bay** is a shopping district with department stores and dining. It is busy but central.

**Sheung Wan** has a mix of traditional shops, galleries, and cafes, slightly calmer than Central.

**Mong Kok** is dense and energetic, with street markets and local flavor, often at lower rates.

## Things to know before you go

- **Entry:** Hong Kong has its own immigration rules, separate from mainland China. US citizens can generally make short visits without a visa. Check official sources before travel.
- **Laws:** Local laws differ from those in the US. Stay informed about local regulations.
- **Tipping:** Many restaurants add a service charge. Additional tipping is optional; rounding up for taxis is common.
- **Etiquette:** Stand on the right of escalators, and queue for transit.
- **Weather:** Typhoon season runs roughly from late spring to fall. Follow official weather warnings, as services may pause.
- **Safety:** Hong Kong is generally safe. Watch belongings in crowded markets. Check official government travel advisories before your trip.

## Getting around

The MTR subway is fast, efficient, and covers most areas. An Octopus card, which also has a mobile version, works on the MTR, buses, trams, ferries, and at many shops. Trams on Hong Kong Island are a slow, cheap way to sightsee. The Airport Express connects the airport to Kowloon and Hong Kong Island quickly. Taxis are plentiful and color-coded by service area.

## Best time to visit

Fall and early winter bring clear skies, lower humidity, and comfortable temperatures. Spring can be humid and foggy. Summer is hot, humid, and rainy, with typhoon risk. Winter is mild and dry.

## Budget tips

- Ride the Star Ferry and trams for cheap sightseeing.
- Eat at local cha chaan tengs (tea restaurants) and dai pai dongs (open-air food stalls) for affordable meals.
- Use an Octopus card for convenience and fare savings.
- Hike free trails for spectacular views.
- Stay in Mong Kok or Wan Chai for better value.
- Visit museums on any free-admission days.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many days do I need in Hong Kong?

Three to four days covers the main sights, with time for an island trip.

### Is Hong Kong good for hiking?

Yes. Country parks cover much of the territory, with trails accessible by public transit.

### Do I need cash?

Cards and Octopus cover most purchases, but some small eateries and markets prefer cash.

### Can I visit Macau from Hong Kong?

Yes, by ferry or the bridge. Check entry requirements, as Macau has its own immigration rules.
