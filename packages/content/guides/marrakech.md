## Why visit Marrakech

Marrakech is a sensory feast of bustling souks, ornate palaces, peaceful gardens, and the famous Jemaa el-Fnaa square, which transforms each evening into a lively scene of food stalls and performers. Its historic medina, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, is a maze of alleys hiding tranquil riads, traditional courtyard houses, many now converted to guesthouses. The city is also a gateway to the Atlas Mountains and desert landscapes, making it a captivating introduction to Morocco.

## Top sights and experiences

- **Jemaa el-Fnaa** — The vibrant main square, especially lively at night.
- **The souks** — A labyrinth of stalls selling crafts, spices, and textiles.
- **Bahia Palace** — Intricate tilework and carved ceilings.
- **Jardin Majorelle** — A striking garden with cobalt-blue buildings.
- **Yves Saint Laurent Museum** — Fashion and design next to the Majorelle garden.
- **Koutoubia Mosque** — Marrakech's landmark minaret, admired from outside.
- **Ben Youssef Madrasa** — A historic Islamic school with stunning architecture.
- **Saadian Tombs** — Ornate royal burial site.
- **Hammam experience** — A traditional bathhouse ritual.
- **Atlas Mountains day trip** — Berber villages, valleys, and mountain views.

## Where to stay: best neighborhoods

**Medina** is the historic heart, filled with riads. It offers an immersive experience but can be hectic and hard to navigate.

**Gueliz** is the modern district, with cafes, shops, and galleries. It's calmer and convenient for transport.

**Hivernage** is upscale, with resorts, restaurants, and nightlife, close to the medina.

**Kasbah** is a quieter part of the medina near the Saadian Tombs, with riads and a local feel.

**Palmeraie** is a palm grove on the outskirts with resorts and villas, ideal for relaxation, but far from the center.

## Things to know before you go

- **Entry:** US citizens can generally visit Morocco for short stays without a visa. Check official sources before you travel.
- **Dress:** Modest clothing, covering shoulders and knees, is respectful, especially in the medina.
- **Mosques:** Most mosques in Morocco are closed to non-Muslim visitors.
- **Photography:** Ask before photographing people. Performers and animal handlers in the square typically expect payment for photos.
- **Bargaining:** Haggling is expected in the souks. Keep it friendly and only bargain if you intend to buy.
- **Tipping:** Around 10 percent in restaurants is common, with small tips for guides and porters.
- **Navigation:** Medina alleys can confuse even map apps. Note landmarks near your riad, and ask your host for directions or an escort on arrival.
- **Mopeds:** Scooters weave through the medina's narrow lanes, so stay alert and keep to the side.
- **Ramadan:** Some restaurants close during the day, and business hours change. Be respectful of those fasting.
- **Safety:** Marrakech is generally safe. Watch for pickpockets in crowded areas. Unofficial guides may offer directions and then ask for payment; politely decline or agree on terms first. Check official government travel advisories before your trip.

## Getting around

The medina is best explored on foot, though it's easy to get lost; offline maps help. Petit taxis serve the city; insist on the meter or agree on a fare first. Horse-drawn carriages are available for leisurely rides. Private drivers and tours are common for day trips. The airport is close to the city.

## Best time to visit

Spring and fall offer warm, pleasant weather. Summer can be extremely hot. Winter is mild during the day but cold at night, with snow on the Atlas Mountains.

## Budget tips

- Eat at food stalls in Jemaa el-Fnaa or local cafes for inexpensive meals.
- Stay in a riad, where breakfast is often included.
- Bargain respectfully in the souks.
- Visit free attractions like the square and mosque exteriors.
- Share private drivers for day trips with others.
- Withdraw dirhams from ATMs, as the currency is generally not available outside Morocco.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many days do I need in Marrakech?

Three to four days covers the medina and gardens, with a day trip to the Atlas Mountains.

### Is tap water safe?

Many visitors prefer bottled water to avoid stomach upset.

### What should I wear in Marrakech?

Light, modest clothing that covers shoulders and knees, plus layers for cooler evenings.

### Can I visit the Sahara from Marrakech?

Yes, but it's a long drive. Multi-day tours are common. The Agafay Desert is a closer, rocky alternative.
