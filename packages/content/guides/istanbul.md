## Why visit Istanbul

Istanbul spans two continents, divided by the Bosphorus strait, and has been the capital of empires for well over a millennium. Byzantine churches, Ottoman mosques, and grand palaces share a skyline with modern towers, and the city's markets, ferries, and street food make every day an adventure. Its neighborhoods range from the historic peninsula to hip cafes on the Asian side, and the food alone, from breakfast spreads to grilled fish, is reason enough to visit.

## Top sights and experiences

- **Hagia Sophia** — A monumental building that has served as church, mosque, and museum, and is now a mosque.
- **Blue Mosque (Sultan Ahmed Mosque)** — Famous for its domes, minarets, and tiled interior.
- **Topkapı Palace** — The opulent seat of Ottoman sultans for centuries.
- **Basilica Cistern** — A vast underground Byzantine water reservoir.
- **Grand Bazaar** — One of the world's oldest covered markets.
- **Spice Bazaar** — Colorful stalls of spices, sweets, and teas.
- **Bosphorus ferry ride** — Public ferries offer sweeping views between continents.
- **Galata Tower** — A medieval tower with panoramic views.
- **Süleymaniye Mosque** — A masterpiece by the architect Sinan, often calmer than the more famous mosques.
- **Kadıköy food market** — Lively streets on the Asian side for eating and browsing.

## Where to stay: best neighborhoods

**Sultanahmet** is the historic core, within walking distance of the major monuments. It suits first-timers, though it is quiet at night and dining is more tourist-oriented.

**Beyoğlu** centers on İstiklal Avenue and Taksim, with shopping, nightlife, and restaurants. It is lively but crowded.

**Karaköy** is a trendy waterfront area with cafes and galleries, conveniently linked by tram to Sultanahmet. It suits design lovers and foodies.

**Beşiktaş** is a local, energetic district along the Bosphorus near Dolmabahçe Palace, with good ferry connections.

**Kadıköy** on the Asian side offers a relaxed, local feel, great food markets, and good value. You will commute by ferry to the main sights, which is part of the charm.

## Things to know before you go

- **Entry:** Visa rules for US citizens visiting Turkey have changed in recent years. Check official sources for current requirements before booking.
- **Mosque etiquette:** Dress modestly; women should cover hair, and everyone should cover shoulders and knees. Remove shoes, and avoid visiting during prayer times.
- **Currency:** Turkey uses the lira. Prices can change frequently, so double-check costs before committing.
- **Tipping:** Around 5 to 10 percent is customary in restaurants if service is not included.
- **Bargaining:** Haggling is normal in bazaars, but keep it friendly.
- **Safety:** Istanbul is generally safe for visitors. Watch for pickpockets in crowded areas. Be cautious if a friendly stranger suggests a particular bar or club, and always check prices before ordering. Use metered taxis or apps. Check official government travel advisories before your trip.

## Getting around

A rechargeable transit card works on trams, Metro, buses, funiculars, and ferries. The T1 tram links Sultanahmet with Karaköy and beyond. Ferries are both transport and sightseeing. Traffic can be heavy, so rail and ferries are often faster than taxis. Istanbul has two major airports: Istanbul Airport on the European side and Sabiha Gökçen on the Asian side, both connected to the city by Metro and bus.

## Best time to visit

Spring and fall offer mild temperatures and comfortable sightseeing. Summer is hot and busy. Winter is cool and damp, with occasional snow, but fewer crowds at the main sights.

## Budget tips

- Use a transit card rather than single tickets.
- Ride public ferries instead of private Bosphorus tours for great views at low cost.
- Eat street food and at local eateries ("lokanta") for hearty, inexpensive meals.
- Visit mosques, which are free to enter outside prayer times.
- Consider a museum pass if you plan to visit several paid sites.
- Stay in Kadıköy or Beşiktaş for better value than Sultanahmet.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many days do I need in Istanbul?

Three to four days covers the historic peninsula, a Bosphorus cruise, and the Asian side.

### Which airport should I fly into?

Istanbul Airport has the most international flights. Sabiha Gökçen can be cheaper, especially for Asian-side stays. Factor in transfer time.

### Can I visit mosques as a tourist?

Yes, outside prayer times and with modest dress. Some major mosques have separate visitor entrances.

### Is tap water safe to drink?

Most locals drink bottled or filtered water, and visitors usually do the same.
