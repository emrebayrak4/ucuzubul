## Why visit Philadelphia

Philadelphia is where the Declaration of Independence and the U.S. Constitution were debated and signed, making it essential for anyone interested in American history. But the city is much more than a living museum. It has world-class art collections, an outstanding public mural program, bustling markets and a dining scene that ranges from classic cheesesteaks to acclaimed restaurants. It is compact, walkable and easy to combine with trips to New York or Washington by train.

## Top sights and experiences

- **Independence Hall** — where the nation's founding documents were adopted; timed tickets may be required.
- **Liberty Bell Center** — the famous cracked bell, displayed across from Independence Hall.
- **Philadelphia Museum of Art** — a major museum, plus the famous steps and nearby Rocky statue.
- **Reading Terminal Market** — a historic indoor market packed with food vendors.
- **Barnes Foundation** — an extraordinary collection of Impressionist and Post-Impressionist art.
- **Museum of the American Revolution** — engaging exhibits on the fight for independence.
- **Eastern State Penitentiary** — a former prison, now a historic site with guided audio tours.
- **Elfreth's Alley** — often described as one of the oldest continuously inhabited residential streets in the country.
- **Murals** — citywide public art, best explored on a guided tour or self-guided route.
- **Italian Market** — one of the oldest outdoor markets in the US, in South Philadelphia.

## Where to stay: best neighborhoods

**Old City** suits history lovers. Pros: next to Independence National Historical Park, cobblestone streets. Cons: busy with tour groups during the day.

**Center City** works for first-timers. Pros: central, many hotels, transit hub. Cons: business-district feel in parts.

**Rittenhouse Square** fits couples and shoppers. Pros: an elegant park, dining and boutiques. Cons: among the more expensive areas.

**Society Hill** appeals to travelers seeking quiet charm. Pros: historic brick streets, near Old City and South Street. Cons: limited hotels.

**Fishtown** is good for food and nightlife fans. Pros: trendy restaurants, breweries, music venues. Cons: a train or ride away from main sights.

## Things to know before you go

- Book Independence Hall tickets in advance during busy seasons through the official National Park Service channels.
- Tipping follows US norms, roughly 15 to 20 percent at restaurants.
- Domestic flyers should confirm their ID meets current TSA rules; international visitors should check official sources for entry requirements.
- Check official government travel advisories before your trip. Use normal city awareness, especially at night and on quieter streets.
- Ordering at classic cheesesteak spots is quick; know your choice of cheese and whether you want onions before you reach the window.
- Street performers and people in costume around landmarks may ask for tips for photos.

## Getting around

Center City and Old City are very walkable. SEPTA runs subways, trolleys, buses and regional rail, including a direct airport line to Center City. You can pay with a SEPTA Key card or contactless options on many routes. Indego bike share is available. Amtrak links 30th Street Station with New York, Washington and Boston, making a car unnecessary for most visits.

## Best time to visit

Spring and fall bring comfortable weather for walking tours. Summer is hot and humid but full of events, especially around the Fourth of July. Winter is cold, quieter and often cheaper.

## Budget tips

- Many historic sites in Independence National Historical Park are free.
- Stay in Center City and walk to most attractions.
- Take regional rail from the airport instead of a taxi.
- Check pay-what-you-wish or discounted evening hours at museums.
- Eat at Reading Terminal Market, food trucks and neighborhood spots.
- Visit in winter or midweek for better hotel rates.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many days do I need in Philadelphia?

Two to three days covers the historic district, a couple of museums and a food crawl.

### Is the Liberty Bell free to visit?

Yes, entry to the Liberty Bell Center is free, though there may be lines and security screening.

### Can I visit Philadelphia as a day trip from New York?

Yes, trains make it possible, but staying at least one night lets you see much more.

### Do I need a car in Philadelphia?

No. Walking, SEPTA and ride-hailing cover visitor needs, and parking downtown can be costly.
