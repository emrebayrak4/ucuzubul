## Why visit Punta Cana

Punta Cana, on the eastern tip of the Dominican Republic, is known for long stretches of palm-lined beaches and a wide range of all-inclusive resorts. Many US cities have direct flights, and the airport sits close to the main resort areas. While many visitors stay put by the pool, the region also offers catamaran trips to nearby islands, snorkeling, cenote-like lagoons, golf and a chance to sample Dominican food, music and hospitality.

The name Punta Cana is often used loosely for the whole stretch of eastern coast, from Uvero Alto in the north to Cap Cana in the south, so check exactly where a resort is located before you book. Distances between areas can be significant, and that affects transfer times, beach conditions and access to restaurants outside your hotel.

## Top sights and experiences

- **Bavaro Beach** — a long, popular stretch of soft sand and calm water.
- **Saona Island** — a day trip by boat to beaches in a national park, often with a stop at a shallow natural pool.
- **Indigenous Eyes Ecological Park** — freshwater lagoons and nature trails near Punta Cana.
- **Hoyo Azul** — a turquoise swimming hole at the base of a cliff in Cap Cana.
- **Macao Beach** — a less-developed beach popular with surfers.
- **Catamaran and snorkel trips** — reef snorkeling and sailing along the coast.
- **Altos de Chavón** — a replica Mediterranean village with views over the Chavón River, near La Romana.
- **Golf** — several well-known oceanfront courses in the area.
- **Merengue and bachata** — dance lessons and live music at resorts and local venues.
- **Santo Domingo day trip** — the historic Colonial Zone, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, a few hours away.

## Where to stay: best neighborhoods

**Bavaro** suits first-timers and families. Pros: the largest concentration of resorts, restaurants and shops. Cons: crowded beaches near big resorts.

**Cap Cana** works for luxury travelers. Pros: upscale resorts, marina, golf. Cons: higher prices and a gated, less local feel.

**Uvero Alto** fits couples seeking seclusion. Pros: quiet beaches and newer resorts. Cons: farther from the airport and attractions.

**Cabeza de Toro** appeals to travelers seeking calm water. Pros: protected beaches, smaller resorts. Cons: limited nightlife.

**Punta Cana Village** is good for value seekers. Pros: near the airport, local restaurants. Cons: no direct beach access.

## Things to know before you go

- US travelers need a valid passport. The Dominican Republic uses an online e-ticket form for entry and exit; check official sources for current requirements.
- Many resorts include tips, but leaving extra for good service is common.
- Check official government travel advisories before your trip, and use resort or licensed transport.
- Drink bottled water and use common sense with street food.
- Beach vendors may be persistent; a polite, firm "no, gracias" usually works.
- Book excursions through reputable operators, and confirm what is included.
- Seaweed can affect some beaches seasonally; ask your resort about conditions.
- Use reef-safe sunscreen.

## Getting around

Most resorts arrange airport transfers, which are the easiest option. Taxis are available but can be pricey; agree on the fare first. Ride-hailing operates in some areas. Local buses (guaguas) are inexpensive but less convenient for visitors. Rental cars are available, but road conditions and driving styles can be challenging.

## Best time to visit

December to April brings sunny, dry weather and the highest prices. May and June can offer value with warm weather. Hurricane season runs from June through November, with the highest risk often in late summer and early fall.

## Budget tips

- Travel in late spring or late fall for better rates.
- Compare all-inclusive packages with flights included.
- Book excursions directly with operators rather than through resort desks.
- Eat at local restaurants outside resorts for authentic Dominican food.
- Bring reef-safe sunscreen and essentials from home, as resort shop prices are higher.
- Use resort shuttles instead of taxis.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a passport to visit Punta Cana?

Yes, US citizens need a valid passport. Check official sources for current entry requirements and forms.

### Is Punta Cana good for families?

Yes. Many resorts have kids' clubs, pools and calm beaches.

### Can I drink the tap water?

It is generally recommended to drink bottled water.

### Is it worth leaving the resort?

Yes, excursions to islands, lagoons and towns offer a fuller sense of the Dominican Republic.
