## Why visit Venice

There is nowhere else like Venice. Built on a lagoon, the city replaces streets with canals and cars with boats, and its palaces, churches, and bridges seem frozen in time. Getting lost in quiet alleys and tiny squares is one of the great pleasures of travel. Venice also offers world-class art, from Renaissance masters to modern collections, and nearby islands known for glassmaking and lacework. Staying overnight lets you see the city at its most tranquil after the day crowds leave.

## Top sights and experiences

- **St. Mark's Basilica** — A glittering Byzantine church covered in mosaics.
- **St. Mark's Square** — The grand heart of Venice.
- **Doge's Palace** — The seat of Venetian power, connected to the Bridge of Sighs.
- **Grand Canal by vaporetto** — The water bus is the most affordable way to see the palaces.
- **Rialto Bridge and market** — An iconic bridge and a lively fish and produce market.
- **Gallerie dell'Accademia** — Venetian painting from the Renaissance and beyond.
- **Peggy Guggenheim Collection** — Modern art in a canal-side palazzo.
- **Murano and Burano** — Islands known for glassblowing and colorful houses.
- **Gondola ride** — A classic, if splurge-worthy, experience.
- **Cicchetti crawl** — Small snacks and wine at traditional bars, called bacari.

## Where to stay: best neighborhoods

**San Marco** is at the center of the action near the main sights. It suits first-timers, though it is crowded and typically the most expensive.

**Cannaregio** is more residential, with lively canalside bars and easy access from the train station. It offers good value and a local feel.

**Dorsoduro** is artsy and relaxed, with major museums and sunny waterfront promenades. It suits culture lovers and couples.

**Castello** is quieter, with authentic neighborhoods and green spaces, though parts are a longer walk from the main sights.

**San Polo** is central and close to the Rialto market, with many restaurants and a bustling atmosphere.

## Things to know before you go

- **Entry:** Italy is part of the Schengen Area. US citizens can generally visit for short stays without a visa, but new EU entry systems are being introduced. Check official sources before you travel.
- **Access fee:** Venice charges day-trippers an access fee on designated peak days. Overnight guests are generally exempt but may need to register. Check the city's official website for current rules.
- **Flooding:** High tides ("acqua alta") can flood low-lying areas, mainly in fall and winter. Sirens and forecasts warn of them, and flood barriers now protect the lagoon in many cases.
- **Rules:** Sitting on monuments, swimming in canals, and picnicking in certain areas are prohibited and can bring fines.
- **Tipping:** Not expected. A cover or service charge may be included.
- **Safety:** Venice is generally safe. Watch for pickpockets in crowded areas and on vaporettos. Check official government travel advisories before your trip.

## Getting around

Venice is car-free. You will walk and use vaporettos (water buses), which run along the Grand Canal and to the islands. Multi-day passes offer much better value than single tickets. Traghetti, gondola ferries, cross the Grand Canal at certain points. From Marco Polo Airport, choose buses to Piazzale Roma or the Alilaguna water bus. Pack light, as there are many bridges with steps.

## Best time to visit

Spring and fall offer pleasant weather and fewer crowds than summer. Summer is hot, humid, and very busy. Winter is cool and misty, with occasional flooding, but it's atmospheric and quieter.

## Budget tips

- Buy a multi-day vaporetto pass if you'll ride more than a few times.
- Stand at the bar for coffee and eat cicchetti instead of full sit-down meals.
- Stay overnight in Cannaregio or Castello, or on the mainland in Mestre for lower rates.
- Fill water bottles at public fountains.
- Many churches are free or inexpensive to enter.
- Avoid restaurants right on St. Mark's Square, where prices are high.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many days should I spend in Venice?

Two to three days lets you explore the main sights and islands and enjoy quieter evenings.

### Is a gondola ride worth it?

It's a splurge but memorable for many. Sharing a gondola with a group lowers the per-person cost.

### Should I stay on the mainland?

Mestre can offer better value, with quick trains and buses to Venice, but you'll miss the city's tranquil evenings.

### How do I pay the access fee?

Check the city's official website for current rules and payment, as requirements and dates can change.
