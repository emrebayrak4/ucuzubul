## Why visit San Juan

San Juan, the capital of Puerto Rico, combines Caribbean beaches with centuries of history. Its colonial old town, founded in the early 1500s, is one of the oldest European-founded cities in the Americas, with colorful buildings, blue cobblestones and massive Spanish forts. Because Puerto Rico is a US territory, the trip is straightforward for US citizens, yet it offers a distinct culture shaped by Taíno, Spanish and African heritage, expressed through music, food and festivals.

## Top sights and experiences

- **Castillo San Felipe del Morro** — a massive Spanish fortress guarding the entrance to San Juan Bay.
- **Castillo San Cristóbal** — another large fort, part of San Juan National Historic Site.
- **Old San Juan streets** — pastel buildings, plazas and the city walls.
- **Paseo de la Princesa** — a waterfront promenade along the old city walls.
- **El Yunque National Forest** — a tropical rainforest with trails and waterfalls, about an hour away; check reservation rules.
- **Condado and Isla Verde beaches** — urban beaches with clear water and resorts.
- **Bioluminescent bays** — glowing waters in Fajardo or on Vieques, best on dark nights.
- **Santurce street art** — vibrant murals and the La Placita market area.
- **Piñones** — roadside kiosks serving traditional fried snacks along a coastal strip.
- **Local music and dance** — salsa, bomba and plena come alive in bars and plazas.

## Where to stay: best neighborhoods

**Old San Juan** suits history lovers and couples. Pros: walkable, charming, near forts and restaurants. Cons: limited beach access and hills.

**Condado** works for first-timers. Pros: beachfront hotels, dining, shopping. Cons: busy and pricier.

**Isla Verde** fits beach lovers and families. Pros: long beach, close to the airport. Cons: farther from Old San Juan.

**Ocean Park** appeals to travelers seeking a residential beach area. Pros: quieter, good for kitesurfing. Cons: fewer large hotels.

**Santurce** is good for food and nightlife fans. Pros: trendy restaurants, art scene, La Placita. Cons: fewer beachfront options.

## Things to know before you go

- US citizens do not need a passport to travel from the mainland, but domestic flyers should confirm their ID meets current TSA rules. Non-US citizens should check official sources.
- The US dollar is the currency, and US cell plans generally work.
- Spanish and English are both official languages; Spanish dominates daily life.
- Tipping follows US norms, around 15 to 20 percent at restaurants.
- Check official government travel advisories before your trip. Use normal city awareness at night and keep valuables out of sight on the beach.
- Beaches can have strong currents; swim where conditions are calm and heed warnings.
- Confirm taxi fares in advance; tourist taxis often use set fares between zones.

## Getting around

Old San Juan is walkable, and a free trolley has historically served parts of it. Taxis and ride-hailing operate in the metro area, though availability can vary. Buses exist but can be infrequent. A rental car is helpful for El Yunque, the east coast and the rest of the island, but parking in Old San Juan is limited, so consider renting only for day trips.

## Best time to visit

Mid-April to June offers warm weather and lower prices after the winter peak. December to March is the high season with drier weather. Hurricane season runs from June through November.

## Budget tips

- Visit after mid-April for lower hotel rates.
- Stay in Isla Verde or Ocean Park for value compared with Condado.
- Explore free beaches and walk around Old San Juan.
- One National Park Service entrance fee has typically covered both forts; check current rules.
- Eat at local cafes and food kiosks.
- Use ride-hailing or public transport instead of taxis where available.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a passport to visit San Juan?

US citizens traveling from the mainland do not need a passport. Check official sources for current rules.

### What currency is used in Puerto Rico?

The US dollar.

### How many days do I need in San Juan?

Three to four days covers Old San Juan, beaches and El Yunque. Add days to visit other islands like Culebra or Vieques.

### Is San Juan good for families?

Yes. Beaches, forts and nature trips appeal to all ages.
