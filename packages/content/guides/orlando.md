## Why visit Orlando

Orlando is one of the world's best-known theme park destinations, home to Walt Disney World, Universal Orlando Resort and SeaWorld. But the region offers more than roller coasters: spring-fed rivers where manatees gather in cooler months, charming small towns, a growing food scene and easy access to Florida's Space Coast. It is a natural pick for families, and adults traveling without kids will find plenty to enjoy too.

## Top sights and experiences

- **Walt Disney World Resort** — four theme parks, two water parks and a huge resort area.
- **Universal Orlando Resort** — thrill rides and immersive themed lands across multiple parks.
- **SeaWorld Orlando** — marine-themed attractions and roller coasters.
- **Kennedy Space Center Visitor Complex** — rockets, astronaut exhibits and launch history, about an hour east.
- **Disney Springs** — a free-to-enter district of shops, restaurants and entertainment.
- **Winter Park** — a leafy town with a scenic boat tour, museums and a walkable main avenue.
- **Blue Spring State Park** — a seasonal refuge for manatees, best visited in winter.
- **Lake Eola Park** — a downtown green space with a fountain and swan boats.
- **ICON Park on International Drive** — a large observation wheel and casual attractions.
- **Gatorland** — an old-school Florida attraction focused on alligators and wildlife.

## Where to stay: best neighborhoods

**Lake Buena Vista** suits Disney-focused families. Pros: proximity to Disney parks, many hotels offer park shuttles. Cons: you may need a car for anything else.

**International Drive** works for travelers splitting time between parks. Pros: central to Universal and SeaWorld, lots of dining, trolley-style transit. Cons: heavy traffic and a commercial feel.

**Kissimmee** fits budget-minded groups and families. Pros: many vacation rentals with pools and kitchens. Cons: spread out, and car-dependent.

**Downtown Orlando** appeals to adults and business travelers. Pros: restaurants, nightlife, arenas. Cons: a drive to the parks.

**Winter Park** is good for a calmer base. Pros: walkable, attractive streets and galleries. Cons: farthest from the parks.

## Things to know before you go

- Theme park tickets, reservations and ride-access systems change regularly; check each park's official website or app before you go.
- Tipping follows US norms, typically around 15 to 20 percent at table-service restaurants.
- International visitors should check official sources for US entry requirements; domestic flyers should confirm their ID meets current TSA rules.
- Check official government travel advisories before your trip, and keep valuables out of sight in rental cars at busy parking lots.
- Be cautious with timeshare presentations offered in exchange for discounted park tickets, and buy tickets from the parks or authorized sellers.
- Florida sun and summer heat are intense; hydrate, use sunscreen and plan breaks.
- Afternoon thunderstorms are common in summer; outdoor rides may pause during lightning.

## Getting around

Orlando is spread out, and a rental car gives the most freedom. Many hotels provide shuttles to theme parks, and Disney runs its own buses, monorail, boats and gondolas within its resort. The I-Ride Trolley serves International Drive, and ride-hailing is widely available. Brightline trains link Orlando International Airport with South Florida. Toll roads are common, so look into your rental car's toll program.

## Best time to visit

Late winter and late fall usually offer pleasant weather and lighter crowds outside of holidays. Summer is hot, humid and busy with school vacations, and hurricane season runs from June through November. Spring break and the winter holidays are the most crowded times.

## Budget tips

- Visit on weekdays outside school holidays, when ticket and hotel demand is lower.
- Multi-day theme park tickets usually cost less per day than single-day tickets.
- A vacation rental with a kitchen can cut food costs for families and groups.
- Bring refillable water bottles; many parks offer free ice water at quick-service counters.
- Compare off-site hotels that offer free park shuttles against on-site perks.
- Mix in free or low-cost activities like Disney Springs, Lake Eola or state parks.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many days do I need in Orlando?

Many families plan a week to cover several parks with rest days. Adults focused on one resort can do a satisfying trip in three or four days.

### Do I need a car in Orlando?

It depends. Staying inside a major resort can make a car optional, but a car helps for grocery runs, other parks and day trips.

### Which airport should I fly into?

Orlando International Airport (MCO) has the most flights. Orlando Sanford (SFB) is smaller and served mainly by low-cost carriers.

### Is Orlando only for theme parks?

No. Springs, lakes, nature preserves, museums and nearby beaches offer a very different side of Central Florida.
