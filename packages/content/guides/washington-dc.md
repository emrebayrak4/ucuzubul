## Why visit Washington, D.C.

The nation's capital combines grand monuments, centers of government and an extraordinary collection of museums, many of them free. Beyond the National Mall, D.C. has leafy residential neighborhoods, a lively food and bar scene and a rich African American cultural history. It is an especially rewarding trip for families, history lovers and anyone interested in how the country works.

## Top sights and experiences

- **National Mall** — the long green corridor linking the Capitol to the Lincoln Memorial.
- **Smithsonian museums** — including Air and Space, Natural History and American History, generally free to enter.
- **National Museum of African American History and Culture** — a powerful museum that often requires timed passes.
- **Lincoln Memorial and Reflecting Pool** — especially striking at dusk and after dark.
- **U.S. Capitol** — tours of the Capitol Visitor Center can be booked in advance.
- **Arlington National Cemetery** — a solemn site just across the Potomac River.
- **Library of Congress** — the ornate Thomas Jefferson Building is worth seeing inside.
- **Georgetown** — historic streets, waterfront and the C&O Canal towpath.
- **National Gallery of Art** — free art spanning centuries, plus a sculpture garden.
- **Tidal Basin memorials** — including the Jefferson and Martin Luther King Jr. memorials.

## Where to stay: best neighborhoods

**Downtown** suits first-time visitors. Pros: walking distance to the Mall, museums and many Metro lines. Cons: quieter on weekends in office-heavy blocks.

**Dupont Circle** works for couples and food lovers. Pros: lively, embassies, restaurants. Cons: a short ride to the Mall.

**Capitol Hill** fits history buffs. Pros: rowhouse streets, Eastern Market, near the Capitol. Cons: fewer hotel choices.

**Georgetown** appeals to shoppers. Pros: charm, waterfront, boutiques. Cons: no Metro station, so you rely on buses or walking.

**Foggy Bottom** is good for quick Mall access. Pros: near the Kennedy Center and Lincoln Memorial, Metro stop. Cons: limited nightlife.

**Navy Yard** suits sports fans and families. Pros: riverfront parks, ballpark, newer hotels. Cons: farther from western monuments.

## Things to know before you go

- Many sights involve security screening, so arrive early and pack light.
- Some popular museums and tours require free timed-entry passes; check official websites beforehand.
- Tipping follows US norms, roughly 15 to 20 percent at table-service restaurants.
- International visitors should check official sources for entry requirements; domestic flyers should verify their ID meets TSA rules, which also matters at some federal buildings.
- Check official government travel advisories before your trip. Use normal city awareness, especially at night.
- On Metro escalators, stand on the right and walk on the left.
- Be wary of unofficial vendors selling "passes" to attractions that are actually free.

## Getting around

The Metro is clean and efficient, with lines serving Reagan National Airport, Dulles via the Silver Line, and most major sights. You can pay with a SmarTrip card, a mobile wallet or a contactless card. Buses, bike share and scooters fill the gaps. The Mall is walkable but large, so wear comfortable shoes. Driving is not recommended because of traffic and limited parking.

## Best time to visit

Spring brings mild weather and the famous cherry blossoms, which also draw big crowds. Fall is pleasant and less busy. Summer is hot and humid, while winter is cool and quiet with lower hotel rates, though weekday rates can rise when Congress is in session.

## Budget tips

- Plan around the many free museums, monuments and memorials.
- Weekend hotel rates in business districts are often lower than weekday rates.
- Pack a picnic for the Mall; food options near the museums can be limited and pricey.
- Use Metro rather than taxis from Reagan National Airport.
- Visit outside cherry blossom season for better hotel availability.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are the Smithsonian museums really free?

Yes, general admission is free at Smithsonian museums in D.C., although some special exhibits, films or timed passes may apply.

### How many days do I need in Washington, D.C.?

Three to four days lets you see the monuments and several museums. Add more days for Mount Vernon or Arlington.

### Which airport is closest to downtown?

Reagan National (DCA) is closest and on the Metro. Dulles (IAD) and Baltimore/Washington (BWI) offer more long-haul routes.

### Can I tour the White House?

Public tours are limited and must be requested in advance through official channels; check current procedures well ahead.
