## Why visit Atlanta

Atlanta is the economic and cultural center of the American South, and it played a defining role in the civil rights movement as the birthplace of Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. Today it is known for its leafy neighborhoods, influential music scene, Southern food and major sports venues. Its airport is one of the busiest in the world, so many travelers pass through; staying a few days reveals a creative, diverse city with plenty to explore.

## Top sights and experiences

- **Martin Luther King Jr. National Historical Park** — King's birth home, Ebenezer Baptist Church and a visitor center.
- **National Center for Civil and Human Rights** — powerful exhibits connecting the civil rights movement to global human rights.
- **Georgia Aquarium** — one of the largest aquariums in the country, with whale sharks.
- **World of Coca-Cola** — the history of the soft drink, plus a famous tasting room.
- **Atlanta BeltLine** — a former rail corridor turned trail lined with art, parks and restaurants.
- **Ponce City Market** — a historic building with food halls, shops and a rooftop.
- **Piedmont Park** — the city's central green space, beside the Atlanta Botanical Garden.
- **High Museum of Art** — a leading art museum in Midtown.
- **Atlanta History Center** — historic homes, gardens and exhibits in Buckhead.
- **Stone Mountain Park** — a huge granite dome with trails and views, east of the city.

## Where to stay: best neighborhoods

**Downtown** suits families and convention-goers. Pros: near the aquarium, Centennial Olympic Park and stadiums. Cons: quieter outside event times.

**Midtown** works for first-timers. Pros: arts district, Piedmont Park, MARTA stations, restaurants. Cons: busy traffic.

**Buckhead** fits shoppers and luxury travelers. Pros: upscale malls, dining, hotels. Cons: car-oriented and spread out.

**Inman Park** appeals to couples. Pros: Victorian homes, BeltLine access, great restaurants. Cons: few hotels.

**Old Fourth Ward** is good for food and culture fans. Pros: BeltLine, Ponce City Market, MLK sites nearby. Cons: limited hotel supply.

**Decatur** suits travelers seeking a small-town feel. Pros: walkable square, MARTA access. Cons: outside the city center.

## Things to know before you go

- Traffic can be heavy, so plan extra time, especially at rush hour.
- Tipping follows US norms, roughly 15 to 20 percent at restaurants.
- Domestic flyers should confirm their ID meets current TSA rules; international visitors should check official sources for entry requirements.
- Check official government travel advisories before your trip. Use normal big-city awareness, and keep belongings out of sight in parked cars.
- People may approach visitors near transit stations or attractions asking for money; a polite "no, thank you" is fine.
- Summers are hot and humid, with frequent afternoon storms.

## Getting around

MARTA trains connect Hartsfield-Jackson Airport with Downtown, Midtown, Buckhead and Decatur, making it easy to arrive without a car. Buses and the Atlanta Streetcar fill in gaps. The BeltLine's Eastside Trail is great for walking, biking or scooters. Many neighborhoods are spread out, so ride-hailing or a rental car helps for wider exploring.

## Best time to visit

Spring brings blooming dogwoods and azaleas and pleasant weather. Fall is mild and comfortable. Summer is hot and humid, while winter is generally mild with occasional cold snaps. Big conventions and sporting events can raise hotel rates.

## Budget tips

- Take MARTA from the airport instead of a taxi.
- Explore free sights like the BeltLine, Piedmont Park and the MLK National Historical Park.
- Compare a multi-attraction pass if you plan to visit several ticketed sights.
- Stay near a MARTA station outside the core for lower rates.
- Avoid big event weekends for better hotel availability.
- Eat at food halls and local Southern spots for value.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many days do I need in Atlanta?

Two to three days covers the major attractions, civil rights sites and a BeltLine walk.

### Is MARTA useful for visitors?

Yes, especially for airport transfers and trips between Downtown, Midtown and Buckhead.

### Is Atlanta good for families?

Yes. The aquarium, children's museums, Zoo Atlanta and parks make it a strong family destination.

### Do I need a car in Atlanta?

Not if you stay near MARTA, but a car helps reach outlying areas like Stone Mountain.
