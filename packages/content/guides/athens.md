## Why visit Athens

Athens is where Western democracy, philosophy, and theater took shape, and the Acropolis still crowns the city as a reminder. Beyond the ancient sites, it is a vibrant, modern capital with rooftop bars, street art, and a food scene built on fresh ingredients and long, social meals. It is also the main gateway to the Greek islands, with ferries departing from nearby ports, making it an ideal start or finish to a broader Greek trip.

## Top sights and experiences

- **Acropolis and Parthenon** — The defining monument of ancient Greece.
- **Acropolis Museum** — A modern museum displaying sculptures from the Acropolis.
- **Ancient Agora** — The marketplace of ancient Athens and the well-preserved Temple of Hephaestus.
- **National Archaeological Museum** — One of the world's great collections of ancient Greek art.
- **Plaka** — Pedestrian lanes of tavernas and neoclassical houses.
- **Temple of Olympian Zeus** — Towering columns of a massive ancient temple.
- **Panathenaic Stadium** — The marble stadium tied to the first modern Olympics.
- **Lycabettus Hill** — The highest viewpoint in central Athens, great at sunset.
- **Monastiraki Flea Market** — Bustling stalls and shops near the square.
- **Day trip to Cape Sounion** — The Temple of Poseidon on a dramatic headland.

## Where to stay: best neighborhoods

**Plaka** is the charming old quarter beneath the Acropolis. It suits first-timers and couples, though it is touristy and can be pricey.

**Koukaki** is a residential area near the Acropolis Museum with cafes and a local feel. It offers good value and walkability.

**Monastiraki** is lively and central, with markets and rooftop bars. It suits those who want energy but can be noisy.

**Syntagma** is the central hub, with direct Metro links to the airport. It is practical, though it can feel more commercial.

**Kolonaki** is upscale, with boutiques and stylish cafes at the foot of Lycabettus. Quieter but more expensive.

**Psyrri** is known for nightlife, street art, and tavernas. Fun at night, with some gritty edges.

## Things to know before you go

- **Entry:** Greece is in the Schengen Area. US citizens can generally visit for short stays without a visa, but new EU entry systems are being introduced. Check official sources before you travel.
- **Heat:** Summer can be extremely hot. Visit archaeological sites early in the morning, bring water and sun protection, and check for heat-related closures.
- **Tipping:** Not obligatory. Rounding up or leaving around 5 to 10 percent for good service is appreciated.
- **Dining:** Greeks often dine late, and meals are relaxed. Sharing plates is common.
- **Strikes:** Transport strikes occasionally happen. Check local news before travel days.
- **Safety:** Athens is generally safe, but pickpocketing is common on the Metro, especially on the airport line, and in crowded areas. Check official government travel advisories before your trip.

## Getting around

The Metro is clean and efficient, connecting the airport, Piraeus port, and central sights. Trams and buses serve the coast and suburbs. Buy tickets or rechargeable cards from machines and validate them. Central Athens is walkable, though sidewalks can be uneven. Taxis are widely available; use the meter or a ride-hailing app. Ferries to the islands leave from Piraeus, Rafina, and Lavrio.

## Best time to visit

Spring and fall are ideal for sightseeing in comfortable temperatures. Summer is very hot and busy, though it's peak island season. Winter is mild and quieter, with shorter opening hours at some sites.

## Budget tips

- Consider a combined ticket covering multiple archaeological sites if you plan to visit several.
- Check official sites for any free-admission days at state sites.
- Eat at casual spots for souvlaki, gyros, and pies, which are filling and inexpensive.
- Ride the Metro rather than taking taxis for airport transfers.
- Book ferries early in peak season for better availability.
- Stay in Koukaki or near Metro stations outside the core tourist zone.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many days do I need in Athens?

Two to three days covers the Acropolis, major museums, and key neighborhoods before heading to the islands.

### When is the best time of day to visit the Acropolis?

Early morning right at opening or late afternoon tends to be cooler and less crowded.

### Is Athens a good base for island trips?

Yes. Ferries from Athens's ports reach many islands, and some are close enough for day trips.

### Can I drink the tap water?

Tap water in Athens is generally considered safe to drink.
