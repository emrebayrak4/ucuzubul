## Why visit Budapest

Budapest straddles the Danube, with hilly Buda on one bank and flat, lively Pest on the other. Its riverfront, lined with grand buildings and spanned by historic bridges, is among the most beautiful in Europe, especially when lit at night. The city is famous for its thermal baths, where locals and visitors soak in mineral-rich waters. Add a creative nightlife scene, hearty Hungarian cuisine, and good value compared with many Western European capitals, and Budapest is hard to beat.

## Top sights and experiences

- **Hungarian Parliament Building** — A Gothic Revival landmark on the riverbank; guided tours available.
- **Buda Castle** — The hilltop palace complex, home to museums and galleries.
- **Fisherman's Bastion** — Fairy-tale terraces with panoramic views.
- **Széchenyi Thermal Bath** — Large outdoor pools in City Park.
- **Gellért Baths** — An Art Nouveau bath complex; check status, as it has undergone renovation.
- **Chain Bridge** — The city's iconic suspension bridge.
- **St. Stephen's Basilica** — A grand church with a dome you can climb.
- **Shoes on the Danube Bank** — A moving Holocaust memorial.
- **Ruin bars** — Eclectic bars in old buildings in the Jewish Quarter.
- **Danube evening cruise** — See the illuminated skyline from the water.

## Where to stay: best neighborhoods

**Belváros** (inner city, District V) is central and walkable, near the river and shopping streets. It suits first-timers, with higher prices.

**Lipótváros** (also District V) surrounds Parliament and St. Stephen's Basilica. It is elegant and convenient, with a slightly calmer feel.

**Jewish Quarter** (part of District VII) is lively, with restaurants, synagogues, and ruin bars. Great for nightlife but noisy on weekends.

**Terézváros** (District VI) runs along grand Andrássy Avenue past the Opera House, with good Metro links and a mix of value and upscale options. Some streets near the bar district get loud at night.

**Castle District** in Buda is romantic and quiet, with views and historic streets. It is less convenient for nightlife and dining.

## Things to know before you go

- **Entry:** Hungary is in the Schengen Area. US citizens can generally visit for short stays without a visa, but new EU entry systems are being introduced. Check official sources before you travel.
- **Currency:** Hungary uses the forint. Pay in forint and decline dynamic currency conversion on card terminals.
- **Tipping:** Around 10 to 15 percent is common in restaurants. Check whether a service charge has already been added to your bill.
- **Bath etiquette:** Bring a swimsuit, flip-flops, and a towel, or rent them. Some pools require swim caps.
- **Safety:** Budapest is generally safe. Watch for pickpockets on crowded public transport and at tourist sites. Check bar menus and prices before ordering, and use official taxis or apps. Check official government travel advisories before your trip.

## Getting around

Budapest has an efficient Metro, plus trams, buses, and trolleybuses. Tram 2 along the Pest riverbank is a scenic ride. Buy tickets or passes from machines or the official app and validate paper tickets before travel; inspectors are common. The center is walkable, though Buda's hills are steep. The airport express bus connects to the city center.

## Best time to visit

Spring and early fall are ideal for sightseeing and outdoor baths. Summer is hot and busy, with lively outdoor venues. Winter is cold, but soaking in an outdoor thermal pool on a chilly day is memorable, and Christmas markets brighten December.

## Budget tips

- Buy a multi-day transit pass if you'll ride often.
- Visit baths on weekdays when they are less crowded.
- Eat lunch specials ("napi menü") for good value.
- Explore the Great Market Hall for affordable local food.
- Enjoy free views from the Castle District grounds and riverbanks.
- Withdraw forint from bank ATMs rather than using exchange booths with poor rates.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many days do I need in Budapest?

Three days covers both sides of the river, a bath, and the nightlife. Add a day for a trip to Szentendre or the Danube Bend.

### Which thermal bath should I choose?

Széchenyi is the largest and most famous. Others offer quieter or more historic experiences. Pick based on atmosphere and current opening status.

### Can I use euros in Budapest?

Some places accept them, but rates are often poor. Use forint or pay by card.

### Is Budapest walkable?

Pest is flat and very walkable. Buda is hilly, so use buses or the funicular to reach the castle.
