## Why visit Madrid

Spain's capital is a city of grand boulevards, leafy parks, and late, lively nights. It holds one of the world's great concentrations of art, with three major museums clustered along a single avenue, and its neighborhood bars make tapas-hopping a way of life. Madrid feels less tourist-focused than some European capitals, which is part of its appeal. It is also a high-speed rail hub, making day trips to Toledo, Segovia, and beyond simple.

## Top sights and experiences

- **Prado Museum** — Masterpieces by Velázquez, Goya, and El Greco.
- **Reina Sofía Museum** — Modern Spanish art, including Picasso's "Guernica."
- **Thyssen-Bornemisza Museum** — A wide-ranging private collection that fills gaps in the other two.
- **Royal Palace** — An opulent official residence with lavish state rooms.
- **Retiro Park** — Rowboats, gardens, and the glass Palacio de Cristal.
- **Plaza Mayor** — A grand arcaded square at the heart of old Madrid.
- **Mercado de San Miguel** — A glass-and-iron market hall for tapas grazing.
- **El Rastro** — A sprawling Sunday flea market in La Latina.
- **Gran Vía** — Madrid's showpiece avenue of theaters and shops.
- **Templo de Debod** — An ancient Egyptian temple and a favorite sunset spot.

## Where to stay: best neighborhoods

**Sol** is the geographic center, ideal for first-timers who want to walk to most sights. Expect crowds and some noise.

**Barrio de las Letras** sits near the big museums with literary history and good restaurants. It is central yet slightly calmer than Sol.

**La Latina** is the place for tapas bars and old-town atmosphere. It suits food lovers but is busy on weekends.

**Malasaña** has a youthful, bohemian vibe with vintage shops and bars. Great for nightlife, though it can be loud.

**Chueca** is vibrant and welcoming, known as the heart of the city's LGBTQ+ scene, with a strong dining and bar culture.

**Salamanca** is upscale and polished, with designer shopping and quieter streets. It is a bit removed from the old-town buzz.

## Things to know before you go

- **Entry:** Spain is in the Schengen Area. US citizens can generally make short visits without a visa, but new EU entry systems are being introduced. Check official sources before you travel.
- **Late hours:** Lunch commonly starts in the early to mid-afternoon and dinner late in the evening. Plan snacks accordingly.
- **Tipping:** Not expected as in the US. Leaving small change or rounding up for good service is common.
- **Summer heat:** July and August can be very hot. Plan outdoor sightseeing for mornings and evenings.
- **Safety:** Madrid is generally safe, but pickpocketing occurs in busy areas such as Sol, Gran Vía, the Metro, and El Rastro. Be wary of distractions, such as someone pointing out a "stain" on your clothing. Check official government travel advisories before your trip.
- **Closures:** Some smaller shops close for a mid-day break, and many museums close one day a week.

## Getting around

The Metro is extensive, clean, and efficient, and buses fill in the gaps. Multi-trip and tourist transit cards offer better value than single tickets. The center is very walkable. From Barajas Airport, the Metro, commuter train, and express airport bus are affordable options; taxis to the center have a fixed fare. High-speed trains depart from Atocha and Chamartín stations.

## Best time to visit

Spring and fall bring pleasant temperatures and outdoor terrace weather. Summer is hot and some locals leave in August, though the city remains lively at night. Winter is cool and sunny on many days, a good time for museums with fewer visitors.

## Budget tips

- The major museums offer free-entry hours on certain days; check official sites and arrive early.
- Order the "menú del día" at lunch for a multi-course meal at a good price.
- Some traditional bars serve a small tapa with drinks.
- Use a multi-trip transit card instead of single tickets.
- Book high-speed trains for day trips well in advance for lower fares.
- Retiro Park and many plazas are free and enjoyable places to spend an afternoon.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many days do I need in Madrid?

Three days covers the main museums, the palace, and a taste of the nightlife. Add a day or two for Toledo or Segovia.

### Is Madrid or Barcelona better for a first trip to Spain?

Both are excellent. Madrid is stronger for art museums and traditional tapas culture, while Barcelona offers beaches and Gaudí architecture. Fast trains connect them.

### Do restaurants open early for dinner?

Some tourist-oriented places do, but most locals dine late. Tapas bars are a good option if you are hungry early.

### Is Madrid walkable?

Yes. The historic center, museums, and Retiro Park are within walking distance of one another.
