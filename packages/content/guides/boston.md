## Why visit Boston

Boston is one of America's oldest cities and played a central role in the American Revolution. Its compact size, brick sidewalks and historic landmarks make it ideal for exploring on foot. Add in leading universities, excellent museums, waterfront seafood and passionate sports fans, and Boston delivers a lot in a short trip, with Cape Cod, Salem and New England's countryside nearby.

## Top sights and experiences

- **Freedom Trail** — a marked route linking historic sites, from Boston Common to the USS Constitution.
- **Boston Common and Public Garden** — the city's oldest park and its famous swan boats in season.
- **Faneuil Hall and Quincy Market** — a historic marketplace with food stalls and street performers.
- **Fenway Park** — the classic home of the Boston Red Sox; tours run year-round.
- **Museum of Fine Arts** — a vast collection spanning ancient to contemporary art.
- **Isabella Stewart Gardner Museum** — art displayed in a Venetian-style palazzo around a courtyard.
- **North End** — Boston's Little Italy, with cafes, pastries and the Paul Revere House.
- **Harvard and Cambridge** — historic campus, bookshops and lively squares across the river.
- **Beacon Hill** — gas lamps, brick rowhouses and the photogenic Acorn Street.
- **Boston Harbor Islands** — seasonal ferries to beaches, trails and a historic fort.

## Where to stay: best neighborhoods

**Back Bay** suits first-timers and shoppers. Pros: elegant streets, Newbury Street, good transit. Cons: among the pricier areas.

**Downtown** works for sightseers. Pros: on the Freedom Trail, central T stations. Cons: some areas quiet down at night.

**North End** fits food lovers. Pros: historic charm, excellent Italian dining. Cons: few hotels and busy streets.

**Seaport District** appeals to business travelers and modern-design fans. Pros: waterfront, new restaurants, convention center. Cons: less historic character.

**Beacon Hill** is good for couples. Pros: quiet, picturesque, near the Common. Cons: limited hotel options and steep streets.

**Cambridge** suits academics and value seekers. Pros: university atmosphere, Red Line to downtown. Cons: a short trip to some Boston sights.

## Things to know before you go

- Boston is very walkable, but many streets follow old colonial paths rather than a grid.
- Tipping follows US norms, roughly 15 to 20 percent at restaurants.
- International visitors should check official sources for US entry requirements; domestic flyers should confirm their ID meets current TSA rules.
- Check official government travel advisories before your trip, and keep an eye on belongings in crowded areas.
- Costumed guides and performers may ask for tips; the official Freedom Trail route can also be walked independently for free.
- Weather changes quickly, and winters can be snowy, so pack layers.

## Getting around

The MBTA subway, known as the T, plus buses and commuter rail cover the city and suburbs. The Silver Line bus runs from Logan Airport to South Station, and the Blue Line connects via a shuttle at Airport station. Water taxis cross the harbor, and Bluebikes is the city's bike share. Driving is stressful, with narrow streets and expensive parking.

## Best time to visit

Late spring and fall are ideal, with mild weather, and autumn brings fall foliage. Summer is warm and busy. Winter is cold and snowy, but hotel rates tend to drop. University graduation weekends and the marathon period can fill hotels quickly.

## Budget tips

- Visit in winter or early spring for lower hotel rates.
- Walk the Freedom Trail on your own using the free markers.
- Look into museum free hours or community days; check each museum's website.
- Stay in Cambridge or near a T station outside downtown.
- Use public transit from the airport instead of taxis.
- Eat at Quincy Market or neighborhood spots rather than waterfront tourist restaurants.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many days do I need in Boston?

Three days covers the Freedom Trail, a museum or two and Cambridge. Add a day for Salem or other day trips.

### Is Boston walkable?

Yes. Most central neighborhoods are close together, and the T fills in longer distances.

### When is the best time to see fall foliage?

Foliage in and around Boston often peaks in October, though timing varies year to year. Check local foliage reports.

### Do I need a car in Boston?

No. A car is only useful for exploring farther afield in New England.
