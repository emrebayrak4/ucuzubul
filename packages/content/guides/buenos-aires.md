## Why visit Buenos Aires

Buenos Aires, Argentina's capital, is a city of grand boulevards, European-style architecture and late-night dining. Known as the birthplace of tango, it has a vibrant cultural life with theaters, bookstores and cafes, plus parrillas (steakhouses) and wine bars. Its neighborhoods each have their own flavor, from cobblestoned San Telmo to leafy Palermo, and porteños (locals) bring a passionate energy to everything from football to food.

The city is large but easy to explore one neighborhood at a time, with leafy plazas, sidewalk cafes and a rhythm that runs late into the night. It is also a convenient gateway to the rest of Argentina, from Patagonia and Iguazú Falls to the wine regions near Mendoza, and to Uruguay across the Río de la Plata.

## Top sights and experiences

- **Recoleta Cemetery** — an elaborate city of mausoleums, including Eva Perón's resting place.
- **Plaza de Mayo and Casa Rosada** — the historic political heart of the city.
- **Teatro Colón** — one of the world's great opera houses; tours offered.
- **San Telmo** — cobblestone streets, antiques and the Sunday street fair.
- **La Boca and Caminito** — brightly painted houses and tango performers; visit in daylight.
- **Palermo Parks** — rose gardens, lakes and the Japanese Garden.
- **El Ateneo Grand Splendid** — a bookstore inside a former theater.
- **MALBA** — Latin American art museum in Palermo.
- **Tango shows and milongas** — from polished dinner shows to local dance halls.
- **Parrillas** — sample Argentina's famous beef and Malbec wines.

## Where to stay: best neighborhoods

**Palermo** suits first-timers and foodies. Pros: restaurants, nightlife, parks. Cons: spread out, and can be noisy.

**Recoleta** works for culture lovers. Pros: elegant, museums, cemetery. Cons: quieter nightlife.

**San Telmo** fits history and tango fans. Pros: charm, antiques, milongas. Cons: some streets feel quiet at night.

**Puerto Madero** appeals to luxury travelers. Pros: modern waterfront, upscale hotels. Cons: less character and pricier dining.

**Belgrano** is good for a residential feel. Pros: quiet, leafy, Chinatown. Cons: farther from main sights.

## Things to know before you go

- Entry rules for US citizens can change; check official Argentine and US government sources before you go.
- Argentina's economy has seen high inflation and currency fluctuations. Exchange rules and card rates change often, so research current options before arrival.
- Check official government travel advisories before your trip. Watch for pickpockets on public transport and in busy areas.
- Dinner is typically eaten late, often after 9 p.m.
- Tipping around 10 percent is common in restaurants.
- Be cautious of distraction scams, like someone pointing out a stain on your clothes.
- Spanish is the official language; local Spanish has distinctive pronunciation and vocabulary.

## Getting around

The Subte (subway) is an efficient way to cross the city, along with an extensive bus network; pay with a SUBE card. Ride-hailing and taxis are widely available. Walking is pleasant in neighborhoods like Palermo and Recoleta. Ezeiza International Airport is well outside the center, so pre-arrange transport or use official airport buses or taxis.

## Best time to visit

Spring (September to November) and fall (March to May) offer mild weather and fewer crowds. Summer (December to February) is hot and humid, and many locals leave on vacation. Winter (June to August) is cool but rarely freezing.

## Budget tips

- Research current exchange options before arrival, since rates can vary significantly by method.
- Enjoy free attractions like parks, street fairs and the cemetery.
- Eat a set lunch menu (menú del día) for value.
- Use public transport and SUBE cards.
- Look for free milongas and tango in the streets of San Telmo.
- Stay in Palermo or Recoleta for good value and walkability.
- Look for free walking tours, which typically work on a tip basis.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many days do I need in Buenos Aires?

Four to five days covers the main neighborhoods and sights. Add days for a trip to Uruguay or wine country.

### Is it safe to visit La Boca?

Stay in the main tourist area of Caminito during daylight, and take a taxi or ride-hail in and out.

### Do I need to speak Spanish?

Basic Spanish helps, though English is spoken in tourist areas.

### What is the best way to pay in Buenos Aires?

Options and rates change frequently. Check recent official and reputable sources before you travel.
