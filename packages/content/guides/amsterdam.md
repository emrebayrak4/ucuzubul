## Why visit Amsterdam

Amsterdam's ring of 17th-century canals, gabled houses, and humpback bridges makes it one of Europe's most picturesque cities. It is compact and flat, so you can see a lot on foot or by bike, and its museums cover everything from Dutch Golden Age masters to Van Gogh. Beyond the postcard center, neighborhoods offer markets, brown cafes, and a creative food scene. The city is also a convenient base for exploring windmills, tulip fields in season, and other Dutch towns.

## Top sights and experiences

- **Rijksmuseum** — Dutch masters, including Rembrandt's "The Night Watch."
- **Van Gogh Museum** — The world's largest collection of the artist's work.
- **Anne Frank House** — A moving visit to the hiding place; tickets are released online only.
- **Canal cruise** — The classic way to see the UNESCO-listed canal ring.
- **Jordaan** — Quiet canals, independent shops, and cozy cafes.
- **Vondelpark** — The city's favorite park for strolling and cycling.
- **Albert Cuyp Market** — A long street market in De Pijp.
- **A'DAM Lookout** — Views over the city from across the IJ river.
- **Begijnhof** — A tranquil historic courtyard hidden in the center.
- **Day trip to Zaanse Schans** — Windmills and traditional wooden houses.

## Where to stay: best neighborhoods

**Canal Belt** puts you amid the most iconic scenery and within walking distance of nearly everything. It suits first-timers and couples but commands premium rates.

**Jordaan** is charming and residential, with boutiques and cafes. It is quieter at night, and rooms are limited.

**De Pijp** is lively and diverse, with great food and the Albert Cuyp Market. It suits foodies and younger travelers and is well connected by Metro and tram.

**Museum Quarter** is elegant and calm, near the major museums and Vondelpark. It is a good choice for families and culture lovers.

**Amsterdam-Noord**, across the IJ, offers newer hotels, creative spaces, and free ferries to Centraal Station. It often has better value but is a little removed from the old center.

## Things to know before you go

- **Entry:** The Netherlands is in the Schengen Area. US citizens can generally visit for short stays without a visa, but new EU entry systems are being introduced. Check official sources before you travel.
- **Bike lanes:** Red-paved paths are for bikes. Don't walk in them, and look both ways before crossing.
- **Payment:** Cards are widely accepted, and some places are card-only. Note that some businesses may not accept every foreign card network.
- **Tipping:** Service is included. Rounding up or leaving a modest extra for good service is customary but optional.
- **Red Light District:** It is a residential neighborhood with rules. Photographing workers is prohibited, and the city has introduced measures to curb nuisance behavior.
- **Safety:** Amsterdam is generally safe. Watch for bike theft and pickpockets in crowded areas and on trams. Be cautious of anyone offering drugs on the street. Check official government travel advisories before your trip.
- **Tourist tax:** Hotels charge a city tourist tax, which is among the higher ones in Europe.

## Getting around

Walk, cycle, or use the tram, Metro, bus, and ferry network run by the local transit authority. You can tap in and out with a contactless card or phone. Always check in and out, or you may be charged a higher fare. Renting a bike is easy, but traffic is fast, so ride confidently and follow local rules. Direct trains from Schiphol reach Centraal Station quickly.

## Best time to visit

Spring brings tulip season in the surrounding region and mild weather. Summer offers long days and outdoor terraces but heavy crowds. Fall is pleasant and quieter. Winter is cold and gray, but cozy cafes and holiday lights add charm.

## Budget tips

- Book museum tickets online, especially for the Anne Frank House, which often sells out.
- Use the free ferries behind Centraal Station to explore Noord.
- Compare a city pass against your plans, since it may include transit and attractions.
- Shop at markets and supermarkets for picnics in the parks.
- Consider staying outside the center, or even in a nearby city with fast train links.
- Rent a bike for a day instead of taking multiple trams.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many days should I spend in Amsterdam?

Two to three days covers the main museums and canals. Add a day for windmills or another Dutch city.

### Do I need to book the Anne Frank House in advance?

Yes. Tickets are sold online in advance only, so book as soon as you know your dates.

### Is it safe to cycle as a visitor?

Many visitors do, but the city's cyclists move fast. If you're not confident, practice in a park first or take a guided bike tour.

### Is English widely spoken?

Yes, English is very widely spoken.
