## Why visit Berlin

Berlin is a city where history is impossible to ignore and creativity is everywhere. You can trace the Cold War along remnants of the Wall, visit world-class museums on a river island, and then spend the evening in a beer garden or a club that runs until morning. Its neighborhoods each have a distinct personality, green space is abundant, and the food scene reflects a diverse, international population. Berlin is also notably spread out, rewarding visitors who explore beyond the center.

## Top sights and experiences

- **Brandenburg Gate** — The neoclassical symbol of a reunified Germany.
- **Reichstag Building** — The parliament's glass dome; free visits require advance registration.
- **Memorial to the Murdered Jews of Europe** — A powerful field of concrete stelae.
- **East Side Gallery** — The longest surviving stretch of the Berlin Wall, covered in murals.
- **Berlin Wall Memorial** — An informative site on Bernauer Strasse showing the border strip.
- **Museum Island** — Five major museums; the Pergamon Museum has been under long-term renovation, so check what is open.
- **Topography of Terror** — A documentation center on the Nazi regime.
- **Checkpoint Charlie area** — The former crossing point, with a nearby museum.
- **Tiergarten** — A vast central park for walks and picnics.
- **Tempelhofer Feld** — A former airport turned open-air park.

## Where to stay: best neighborhoods

**Mitte** is central and close to the major landmarks and museums. It suits first-timers, though it can feel more polished and touristy than other areas.

**Prenzlauer Berg** is leafy and residential, with cafes, playgrounds, and weekend markets. It is ideal for families and couples seeking calm.

**Kreuzberg** is diverse and energetic, known for street food, canalside bars, and nightlife. It suits younger travelers, with some lively, noisy streets.

**Friedrichshain** is a nightlife hub near the East Side Gallery, with a casual, alternative vibe and often good value.

**Charlottenburg** in the west is elegant, with Charlottenburg Palace and upscale shopping along the Kurfürstendamm, but it is farther from the eastern nightlife.

## Things to know before you go

- **Entry:** Germany is in the Schengen Area. US citizens can generally visit for short stays without a visa, but new EU entry systems are being introduced. Check official sources before you travel.
- **Cash:** Card acceptance has grown, but some cafes, bars, and small shops still prefer cash. Carry a little.
- **Tipping:** Rounding up or adding about 5 to 10 percent for table service is common. Tell the server the total you want to pay rather than leaving money on the table.
- **Sundays:** Most shops close on Sundays, though restaurants, museums, and some convenience stores stay open.
- **Etiquette:** Wait for the green pedestrian light even when the road is empty, and keep bike lanes clear.
- **Safety:** Berlin is generally safe. Watch for pickpockets on crowded trains and at major sights, and be cautious of street games like shell games. Check official government travel advisories before your trip.

## Getting around

Berlin's U-Bahn, S-Bahn, trams, and buses form an extensive network. Tickets cover zones; most sights are in zones A and B, while the airport is in zone C. Validate paper tickets before boarding, as plainclothes inspectors check regularly. Day passes and multi-day travel cards can save money. Cycling is popular and the city is mostly flat. Trains connect Berlin Brandenburg Airport with the city center.

## Best time to visit

Late spring through early fall brings warm weather, outdoor cafes, and open-air events. Summer is the busiest season. Winter is cold and dark but offers Christmas markets in December and fewer crowds at museums.

## Budget tips

- Many memorials and historic sites are free, including the Wall Memorial and the Reichstag dome (with registration).
- Buy a day or multi-day transit ticket rather than single fares.
- Eat street food, such as döner kebab or currywurst, for cheap, filling meals.
- Consider a museum pass if you plan to visit several museums on Museum Island.
- Stay in Friedrichshain or Kreuzberg for generally better value than Mitte.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many days do I need in Berlin?

Three to four days covers the main historic sites and museums. Add a day for a trip to Potsdam.

### Is Berlin's nightlife accessible to visitors?

Yes, though some famous clubs have strict, unpredictable door policies. Many bars and smaller venues are more relaxed.

### Do I need to book the Reichstag dome?

Yes. Visits require advance registration through the parliament's official website.

### Is English widely spoken in Berlin?

Yes, especially among younger people and in the hospitality industry.
