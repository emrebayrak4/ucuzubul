## Why visit Lima

Lima, Peru's capital, sits on cliffs above the Pacific Ocean and is widely regarded as one of the leading food cities in Latin America. Its cuisine blends Indigenous, Spanish, African, Chinese and Japanese influences, from fresh ceviche to Nikkei and chifa dishes. The city also has a UNESCO-listed historic center, ancient adobe pyramids within city limits and a bohemian arts district. Many travelers pass through on the way to Cusco, but Lima deserves a few days of its own.

The city is spread along a long stretch of coastline, and the districts most visitors use, Miraflores, Barranco and San Isidro, sit close together near the ocean. Surfers ride the waves below the cliffs, cyclists follow the clifftop paths, and evenings revolve around long dinners. Lima also works well as a starting point for wider trips to the Andes, the Amazon and the desert coast to the south.

## Top sights and experiences

- **Historic Center of Lima** — colonial architecture around Plaza Mayor, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **Monastery of San Francisco** — a baroque church with catacombs and a historic library.
- **Larco Museum** — pre-Columbian art and ceramics in a colonial mansion with gardens.
- **Huaca Pucllana** — an adobe pyramid complex in the middle of Miraflores.
- **Malecón of Miraflores** — clifftop parks and paths with ocean views and paragliding.
- **Barranco** — colorful houses, street art, galleries and the Bridge of Sighs.
- **Ceviche and food tours** — sample Lima's signature dishes and markets.
- **Parque de la Reserva Magic Water Circuit** — an evening fountain park with light shows.
- **Pachacamac** — a vast archaeological site south of the city.
- **Surquillo Market** — a local market for tropical fruits and Peruvian ingredients.

## Where to stay: best neighborhoods

**Miraflores** suits first-timers. Pros: oceanfront parks, restaurants, many hotels. Cons: busy and more touristy.

**Barranco** works for creative types and nightlife fans. Pros: art, bars, bohemian vibe. Cons: smaller hotel supply.

**San Isidro** fits business travelers. Pros: quiet, green, upscale dining. Cons: less nightlife.

**Historic Center** appeals to history buffs. Pros: near colonial landmarks. Cons: quieter at night and farther from the coast.

## Things to know before you go

- US travelers need a valid passport; check official sources for current entry rules.
- Check official government travel advisories before your trip. Petty theft can occur, so watch your belongings in crowded areas and keep phones secure.
- Tipping around 10 percent is common at restaurants if service is not included.
- Drink bottled or filtered water.
- Use ride-hailing apps or registered taxis rather than hailing cars on the street.
- Spanish is the main language; basic phrases help.
- The coastal fog (garúa) makes winter skies gray much of the time.
- Restaurants at the top end of the dining scene often require reservations weeks ahead; casual cevicherías are typically busiest at lunch, since ceviche is traditionally a daytime dish.

## Getting around

Lima is large and traffic is heavy, so it pays to base yourself near the places you most want to see. The Metropolitano bus rapid transit links Barranco, Miraflores and the historic center. Ride-hailing apps are widely used and convenient. Walking is pleasant in Miraflores and Barranco. Allow plenty of time for airport trips, especially at rush hour.

## Best time to visit

Lima's summer, December to April, brings sunshine and warm weather. Winter, June to September, is mild but often overcast and humid. The city receives very little rain throughout the year.

## Budget tips

- Eat set lunch menus (menú) for affordable, filling meals.
- Visit markets for fruit, snacks and local dishes.
- Use the Metropolitano instead of taxis for longer trips.
- Explore free sights like the Malecón and Barranco streets.
- Stay in Miraflores or Barranco to walk to many attractions.
- Book restaurants in advance, since popular spots fill up.
- Fly to Cusco or other regions midweek, when domestic fares are often lower.
- Ask whether a service charge is already included before tipping.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many days do I need in Lima?

Two to three days covers the main sights and a few memorable meals.

### Is Lima worth visiting or just a stopover?

It is worth a stay, especially for food lovers and history fans.

### Can I drink the tap water in Lima?

It is generally recommended to drink bottled or filtered water.

### How do I get from Lima to Cusco?

Most travelers fly, as the overland journey is long.
