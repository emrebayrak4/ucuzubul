## Why visit Lisbon

Lisbon spreads across hills above the wide Tagus River, with tiled facades, pastel buildings, and viewpoints around nearly every corner. It is one of Europe's sunniest capitals, with a relaxed pace, excellent seafood, and a soulful music tradition in fado. History runs deep here, from the Moorish lanes of Alfama to the monuments of Portugal's Age of Discovery in Belém. Beaches and the fairy-tale palaces of Sintra are an easy day trip away.

## Top sights and experiences

- **Alfama** — The oldest quarter, a maze of lanes, laundry lines, and fado houses.
- **São Jorge Castle** — Hilltop ramparts with sweeping views.
- **Belém Tower and Jerónimos Monastery** — Landmarks of Portugal's maritime era.
- **Tram 28** — A classic vintage tram route through historic neighborhoods.
- **Miradouros** — Viewpoints like Senhora do Monte and Santa Luzia.
- **LX Factory** — Shops, cafes, and street art in a former industrial complex.
- **Pastéis de nata** — Custard tarts, famously associated with Belém.
- **Fado performance** — An evening of traditional Portuguese song.
- **National Tile Museum** — The history and artistry of azulejos.
- **Day trip to Sintra** — Romantic palaces and gardens in wooded hills.

## Where to stay: best neighborhoods

**Baixa** is the flat, central grid rebuilt after the 1755 earthquake. It is convenient for transit and first-timers, though it can feel commercial.

**Chiado** offers elegant streets, cafes, and theaters between Baixa and Bairro Alto. It is central and stylish, usually at higher rates.

**Alfama** delivers atmosphere and views, ideal for romantic stays. Expect steep, uneven streets and limited car access.

**Bairro Alto** is the nightlife hub, packed with small bars. It's fun for night owls but noisy late into the night.

**Príncipe Real** is leafy and upscale with boutiques and gardens, quieter yet still walkable.

**Cais do Sodré** sits by the river with lively bars and train access to the beaches of Cascais.

## Things to know before you go

- **Entry:** Portugal is in the Schengen Area. US citizens can generally visit for short stays without a visa, but new EU entry systems are being introduced. Check official sources before you travel.
- **Hills and shoes:** Lisbon's hills and slick limestone sidewalks call for shoes with good grip.
- **Tipping:** Modest. Rounding up or leaving a small amount for good service is appreciated but not required.
- **Couvert:** Bread, olives, or other starters brought to the table may be charged if eaten. You can politely decline them.
- **Safety:** Lisbon is generally safe. Watch for pickpockets on crowded trams and at viewpoints. You may be offered drugs on the street in tourist areas; simply decline and walk on. Check official government travel advisories before your trip.
- **Tourist tax:** Accommodations charge a per-night city tax.

## Getting around

The Metro is simple and efficient, while trams, buses, funiculars, and elevators help with the hills. A rechargeable transit card covers most public transport; contactless options are also expanding. Tram 28 is popular and crowded, so ride early or try other tram routes. Trains run to Sintra and Cascais from central stations. The airport is close to the city and connected by Metro.

## Best time to visit

Spring and early fall offer warm, sunny days with fewer crowds. Summer is hot and busy, especially in popular neighborhoods. Winters are mild but can be rainy; it's a quieter, cheaper time to visit.

## Budget tips

- Use a rechargeable transit card rather than paying onboard.
- Look for the "prato do dia" (dish of the day) at local restaurants for a filling, affordable meal.
- Explore the free miradouros instead of paid viewing attractions.
- Visit Sintra on a weekday and start early to beat crowds.
- Compare a city tourist card against your planned sights before buying.
- Stay a little outside the center near a Metro line for better rates.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many days do I need in Lisbon?

Three to four days covers the main neighborhoods and Belém, with one day for Sintra.

### Is Lisbon walkable?

Yes, but it's hilly. Mix walking with trams, funiculars, and the Metro to save your legs.

### Can I swim near Lisbon?

Yes. Beaches along the Cascais line and across the river are reachable by train or ferry and bus.

### What should I eat in Lisbon?

Try grilled sardines in season, bacalhau (salt cod) dishes, bifanas (pork sandwiches), and pastéis de nata.
