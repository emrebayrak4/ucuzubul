## Why visit Toronto

Toronto is Canada's largest city and one of the most multicultural cities in the world. That diversity shows up in its neighborhoods and its food, from Chinatown and Little Italy to Greektown and Little Portugal. Set on Lake Ontario, the city pairs a dramatic skyline with islands, beaches and parks. Strong museums, theater, sports and easy access to Niagara Falls make it a great short trip from the US.

## Top sights and experiences

- **CN Tower** — the city's signature tower with glass floors and panoramic views.
- **Toronto Islands** — a short ferry ride to beaches, bike paths and skyline views.
- **Royal Ontario Museum** — natural history, world cultures and art in a striking building.
- **St. Lawrence Market** — a historic food market with local specialties.
- **Distillery District** — Victorian industrial buildings turned pedestrian village of shops and galleries.
- **Kensington Market** — an eclectic neighborhood of vintage shops, cafes and street art.
- **Art Gallery of Ontario** — Canadian and international art in a building by Frank Gehry.
- **Ripley's Aquarium of Canada** — a family favorite at the foot of the CN Tower.
- **Casa Loma** — a castle-style mansion with gardens and city views.
- **Niagara Falls** — a popular day trip about a 90-minute drive away, traffic permitting.

## Where to stay: best neighborhoods

**Downtown** suits first-timers. Pros: central, near the waterfront, subway access. Cons: busy and business-oriented in places.

**Entertainment District** works for theater and sports fans. Pros: near the CN Tower, theaters and arenas. Cons: crowded on event nights.

**Yorkville** fits luxury travelers and shoppers. Pros: upscale boutiques, galleries, close to the ROM. Cons: higher prices.

**Distillery District** appeals to couples. Pros: charming cobblestone lanes, restaurants. Cons: farther from subway lines.

**Queen West** is good for creative types. Pros: independent shops, bars, galleries. Cons: fewer large hotels.

**The Annex** suits budget-minded and academic visitors. Pros: near the University of Toronto, cafes, subway access. Cons: mostly small inns and rentals.

## Things to know before you go

- US citizens flying to Canada need a valid passport; land and sea travelers have other document options. Check official sources for current requirements.
- Tipping is similar to the US, typically 15 to 20 percent at restaurants.
- Prices are usually shown before sales tax, which is added at checkout.
- Check official government travel advisories before your trip, and use normal city awareness in busy areas.
- Winters are cold and snowy; the underground PATH walkway network links many downtown buildings.
- Be wary of unofficial ticket resellers for sports and concerts; use official sellers.
- Canadians tend to be polite and orderly; queueing is expected.

## Getting around

The TTC runs subways, streetcars and buses across the city, and you can pay with a PRESTO card or a contactless bank card or phone. The UP Express train links Pearson Airport with Union Station. Billy Bishop Airport, on the Toronto Islands, serves regional flights and is close to downtown. Bike Share Toronto is popular in warm months. A car is helpful mainly for day trips.

## Best time to visit

Late spring through early fall brings warm weather, patios and festivals. Fall foliage can be lovely. Winter is cold but offers skating, holiday markets and lower hotel rates. Summer is the busiest season.

## Budget tips

- Visit in winter or early spring for lower hotel rates.
- Take the UP Express or TTC from Pearson instead of a taxi.
- Explore free attractions like Kensington Market, the waterfront and many parks.
- Consider a city attraction pass if visiting several paid sights.
- Check museum free or discounted evening hours.
- Eat in neighborhoods like Chinatown and Koreatown for great value.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a passport to visit Toronto?

US citizens flying to Canada need a valid passport. Check official Canadian and US government sources for current rules.

### How many days do I need in Toronto?

Three to four days covers the main sights and a Niagara Falls day trip.

### Can I use US dollars in Toronto?

Some places may accept them, but at unfavorable rates. Use Canadian currency or a card without foreign transaction fees.

### Is Toronto good for families?

Yes. The islands, aquarium, science centre and museums are great for kids.
