## Why visit Florence

Florence was the cradle of the Renaissance, and the compact city still holds an extraordinary concentration of art and architecture. Within a short walk you can see Brunelleschi's dome, Michelangelo's David, and galleries filled with masterpieces by Botticelli, Leonardo, and Raphael. Beyond the art, Florence is a food lover's city, known for Tuscan staples like ribollita, bistecca, and gelato, and it makes an ideal base for day trips to Tuscan hill towns and vineyards.

## Top sights and experiences

- **Florence Cathedral (Duomo)** — Brunelleschi's dome; climbing it requires advance booking.
- **Uffizi Gallery** — One of the world's great collections of Renaissance art.
- **Galleria dell'Accademia** — Home of Michelangelo's David.
- **Ponte Vecchio** — The medieval bridge lined with jewelers' shops.
- **Piazza della Signoria and Palazzo Vecchio** — The civic heart of the city, with outdoor sculpture.
- **Piazzale Michelangelo** — The classic panoramic viewpoint over the city.
- **Basilica of Santa Croce** — Tombs of Michelangelo, Galileo, and others.
- **Pitti Palace and Boboli Gardens** — A grand palace with museums and terraced gardens.
- **Mercato Centrale** — A food market with a lively upstairs food hall.
- **Day trip to Chianti, Siena, or San Gimignano** — Tuscan landscapes and medieval towns.

## Where to stay: best neighborhoods

**Duomo** puts you at the very center, within walking distance of everything. It suits first-timers, though it is crowded and pricey.

**Santa Croce** offers a lively area with restaurants and nightlife near the basilica. It is central with a more local feel in places.

**Oltrarno**, across the river, is known for artisan workshops, quieter piazzas, and authentic trattorias. It suits repeat visitors and couples.

**Santa Maria Novella** is convenient to the main train station, making it practical for day trips. Some streets near the station are less charming.

**San Lorenzo** surrounds the central market, with good value options and easy access to the Accademia. Market streets can be busy and touristy.

## Things to know before you go

- **Entry:** Italy is part of the Schengen Area. US citizens can generally visit for short stays without a visa, but new EU entry systems are being introduced. Check official sources before you travel.
- **Book ahead:** Timed tickets for the Uffizi, Accademia, and Duomo climbs often sell out, especially in high season.
- **Dress codes:** Cover shoulders and knees when entering churches.
- **Tipping:** Not expected as in the US. A cover charge may appear on the bill; rounding up for good service is appreciated.
- **Limited traffic zones:** Much of the center restricts car access, and violations can bring fines. Avoid driving into the historic center.
- **City tax:** Accommodations charge a nightly tourist tax.
- **Safety:** Florence is generally safe. Watch for pickpockets in crowded areas and on buses. Check official government travel advisories before your trip.

## Getting around

Florence's historic center is compact and best explored on foot. Buses and a modern tram network serve the outskirts and airport. Florence's small airport is linked by tram to the city, and many travelers also use Pisa airport, connected by train and bus. Trains from Santa Maria Novella station reach Rome, Venice, and Milan quickly.

## Best time to visit

Spring and fall offer pleasant weather and long days for sightseeing. Summer is hot and crowded. Winter is cooler and quieter, with shorter lines at museums and lower hotel rates.

## Budget tips

- Book museum tickets online to avoid lines and secondhand resellers.
- Check whether state museums have free-admission days and plan accordingly.
- Eat lunch at the Mercato Centrale or try a lampredotto or schiacciata sandwich for a cheap, local meal.
- Stay in Oltrarno or near the train station for better rates.
- Use regional trains for day trips instead of tours where practical.
- Enjoy free views from Piazzale Michelangelo and church exteriors.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many days should I spend in Florence?

Two to three days covers the main museums and sights. Add days for Tuscany day trips.

### Do I need to book tickets for David?

Booking a timed entry for the Accademia is strongly recommended to avoid long waits.

### Is Florence walkable?

Yes. Nearly every major sight is within a short walk in the historic center.

### Should I rent a car in Florence?

Not for the city itself. A car can be useful for exploring the Tuscan countryside, but pick it up on the outskirts to avoid restricted zones.
