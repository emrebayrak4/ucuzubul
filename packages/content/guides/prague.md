## Why visit Prague

Prague's skyline of spires, its castle above the Vltava River, and its remarkably intact old town make it one of Europe's most beautiful capitals. Centuries of architecture, from Gothic to baroque to Art Nouveau, survive in a compact center that is a joy to explore on foot. Czech beer culture is a draw in its own right, and hearty local food fuels long days of walking. Evenings on the riverbanks or in a cozy pub are part of the experience.

## Top sights and experiences

- **Prague Castle** — A vast castle complex including St. Vitus Cathedral.
- **Charles Bridge** — The statue-lined medieval bridge; go early to beat crowds.
- **Old Town Square and Astronomical Clock** — The city's historic heart.
- **Jewish Quarter (Josefov)** — Historic synagogues and an old cemetery.
- **Lesser Town (Malá Strana)** — Baroque streets and palaces beneath the castle.
- **Petřín Hill** — Gardens, a lookout tower, and city views.
- **Vyšehrad** — A historic fortress with a cemetery and quieter views.
- **Municipal House** — An Art Nouveau landmark with a grand cafe.
- **Beer halls and microbreweries** — A cornerstone of Czech culture.
- **Riverside walks and paddle boats** — A relaxed way to see the city from the Vltava.

## Where to stay: best neighborhoods

**Old Town** is central and atmospheric, close to major sights. It suits first-timers but can be crowded and touristy, especially near the main square.

**Lesser Town** offers romantic streets near the castle and Charles Bridge. Quieter at night, though it has many cobblestones and hills.

**New Town** is central and practical, with more hotel variety and good transit connections, including around Wenceslas Square.

**Vinohrady** is a leafy residential area with cafes, wine bars, and parks. It suits travelers who want a local feel, a short tram ride from the center.

**Karlín** is a revitalized district with modern hotels and a growing dining scene, convenient by Metro.

## Things to know before you go

- **Entry:** The Czech Republic is in the Schengen Area. US citizens can generally visit for short stays without a visa, but new EU entry systems are being introduced. Check official sources before you travel.
- **Currency:** The country uses the Czech koruna, not the euro. Some places accept euros but at unfavorable rates.
- **Money exchange:** Avoid street money changers and compare rates carefully at exchange offices. Withdrawing from bank ATMs and declining "dynamic currency conversion" is usually the better choice.
- **Tipping:** Rounding up or adding around 10 percent for good table service is common.
- **Taxis:** Use reputable ride-hailing apps or official taxi stands to avoid overcharging.
- **Safety:** Prague is generally safe, but pickpocketing happens in crowded areas and on trams. Check official government travel advisories before your trip.

## Getting around

The historic center is best explored on foot. Trams cover the city well, the Metro is fast for longer distances, and the funicular climbs Petřín Hill. Buy tickets from machines or apps and validate paper tickets before boarding; inspectors check regularly. Short-term passes are good value. From Václav Havel Airport, buses connect to the Metro, and an airport express bus runs to the main train station.

## Best time to visit

Spring and early fall offer comfortable temperatures and blooming or golden parks. Summer is busy, especially on Charles Bridge. Winter is cold, but December brings Christmas markets to the main squares.

## Budget tips

- Explore beyond the Old Town for lower prices on food and drink.
- Buy a multi-day transit pass instead of single tickets.
- Many churches and gardens are free or inexpensive to enter.
- Order the daily lunch menu ("denní menu") at local restaurants for good value.
- Withdraw local currency from bank ATMs rather than exchanging cash in tourist areas.
- Visit major sights early or late to avoid paying for skip-the-line options.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many days do I need in Prague?

Three days is ideal for the main sights. Add a day for a trip to Kutná Hora or Český Krumlov.

### Can I pay with euros in Prague?

Sometimes, but it's not standard and the rate is often poor. Paying in koruna or by card is better.

### Is Prague walkable?

Very. The castle area involves climbing, but trams can help with hills.

### Is tap water safe in Prague?

Yes, tap water is considered safe to drink.
