## Why visit Honolulu

Honolulu, on the island of Oahu, is Hawaii's capital and largest city. It pairs a famous beach district with green volcanic mountains, important historic sites and a food culture shaped by Native Hawaiian, Asian and Pacific traditions. You can surf, hike a crater, learn about Hawaiian royalty and still be back for sunset on the sand. Oahu's North Shore and windward beaches are a scenic drive away.

## Top sights and experiences

- **Waikiki Beach** — gentle waves for beginner surfers and a lively beachfront promenade.
- **Pearl Harbor National Memorial** — the USS Arizona Memorial and related historic sites; reserve program tickets in advance.
- **Diamond Head State Monument** — a crater hike with panoramic views; out-of-state visitors need reservations.
- **Iolani Palace** — the only royal palace on US soil, home to Hawaii's last monarchs.
- **Hanauma Bay Nature Preserve** — snorkeling in a protected bay; reservations and an educational video are required.
- **Bishop Museum** — Hawaii's leading museum of natural and cultural history.
- **North Shore** — legendary winter surf, shrimp trucks and the town of Haleiwa.
- **Manoa Falls Trail** — a short rainforest walk to a waterfall.
- **Kailua and Lanikai beaches** — soft sand and calm, turquoise water on the windward side.
- **Chinatown and local food** — try poke, plate lunches, shave ice and malasadas.

## Where to stay: best neighborhoods

**Waikiki** suits first-timers and families. Pros: beach, walkable dining and shopping, huge hotel supply. Cons: crowded and touristy.

**Ala Moana** works for shoppers and value seekers. Pros: near a big mall and beach park, often lower rates. Cons: a walk or bus ride to Waikiki's center.

**Downtown Honolulu** fits history and business travelers. Pros: near Iolani Palace and Chinatown. Cons: limited beach access and quieter evenings.

**Ko Olina** is good for a resort-style stay. Pros: calm lagoons, sunsets, resort amenities. Cons: far from the city; a car is essential.

**Kailua** appeals to beach lovers seeking a slower pace. Pros: stunning beaches, small-town feel. Cons: mostly vacation rentals, so check local rental rules.

## Things to know before you go

- No passport is needed for US citizens flying from the mainland, but domestic flyers should confirm their ID meets current TSA rules. International visitors should check official sources.
- When leaving Hawaii for the mainland, bags go through agricultural inspection; some fruits and plants are restricted.
- Check official government travel advisories before your trip, and never leave valuables in a parked car at trailheads or beaches.
- Respect wildlife: keep your distance from monk seals and sea turtles, which are protected by law.
- Use reef-safe sunscreen; Hawaii restricts certain sunscreen ingredients.
- Respect sacred sites, private property and closed trails.
- Timeshare presentations may be offered with discounted activities; consider the time commitment.
- Tipping follows US norms.

## Getting around

TheBus covers much of Oahu, and Skyline rail serves parts of the west side of Honolulu. Waikiki is walkable, and bike share is available. Ride-hailing and taxis are common. A rental car makes it easier to explore the North Shore and windward coast, but hotel parking in Waikiki can be expensive, so consider renting for only part of your stay.

## Best time to visit

Honolulu is warm year-round. Spring and fall typically bring fewer crowds and good weather. Winter is the rainier season and brings big surf to the North Shore, while summer is drier with calmer north-facing waters. Holidays and school vacations are busiest.

## Budget tips

- Travel in spring or fall, avoiding major holidays, for better fares and rates.
- Rent a car for just a day or two instead of the whole trip.
- Eat at plate lunch spots, food trucks and grocery store poke counters.
- Enjoy free beaches, sunsets and many hiking trails.
- Book attractions with limited reservations early to avoid pricey tours.
- Compare condo-style rentals with kitchens for longer stays.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many days do I need in Honolulu?

Four to five days lets you see Honolulu's highlights and spend a day on the North Shore. A week allows a relaxed pace.

### Do I need a passport to visit Honolulu?

US citizens traveling from the mainland do not need a passport. International travelers should follow US entry rules.

### Can I visit Pearl Harbor without a reservation?

Some areas are open without a reservation, but the USS Arizona Memorial program uses timed tickets that often run out. Book ahead through the official site.

### Is Waikiki good for swimming?

Yes, much of Waikiki has gentle conditions, but always check lifeguard flags and posted warnings.
