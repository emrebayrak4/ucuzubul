## Why visit Bali

Bali is an Indonesian island known for its emerald rice terraces, volcanic peaks, surf beaches, and a distinctive Hindu culture expressed through temples, ceremonies, and daily offerings. It suits a remarkable range of travelers: surfers, yogis, honeymooners, families, and food lovers all find their niche. You can spend the morning at a temple, the afternoon on a beach, and the evening at a clifftop restaurant watching the sunset. Bali's warmth, both in climate and hospitality, keeps visitors returning.

## Top sights and experiences

- **Tegallalang Rice Terraces** — Classic terraced landscapes near Ubud.
- **Uluwatu Temple** — A clifftop temple with sunset Kecak dance performances.
- **Tanah Lot** — A sea temple on a rocky outcrop.
- **Tirta Empul** — A water temple with sacred purification pools.
- **Sacred Monkey Forest Sanctuary** — Forest temples in Ubud, home to many macaques.
- **Mount Batur sunrise trek** — A pre-dawn hike up an active volcano.
- **Nusa Penida** — Dramatic cliffs and snorkeling, by boat from Bali.
- **Seminyak beaches** — Sunsets, beach clubs, and dining.
- **Balinese cooking class** — Learn local dishes and market ingredients.
- **Traditional dance performance** — An evening of Legong or Barong dance in Ubud.

## Where to stay: best neighborhoods

**Seminyak** offers upscale dining, shopping, and beach clubs. It suits couples and those seeking nightlife, though traffic can be heavy.

**Ubud** is the cultural heart, set among rice fields and jungle, with yoga and wellness retreats. It's inland, so no beach.

**Canggu** is popular with surfers and digital nomads, with cafes and a laid-back vibe. Rapid development means construction and traffic in places.

**Uluwatu** offers clifftop views, surf breaks, and dramatic beaches. It is more spread out and best with a driver or scooter.

**Sanur** is calm and family-friendly, with a beachfront path and gentle waters.

**Nusa Dua** is a resort enclave with manicured beaches, ideal for families seeking convenience and calm.

## Things to know before you go

- **Entry:** Indonesia's visa rules for US citizens and Bali's tourist levy can change. Check official sources for current requirements before you travel.
- **Temple etiquette:** Wear a sarong and sash, which are often available to borrow or rent. Don't step on offerings on the ground, and follow posted rules.
- **Nyepi:** On the Balinese Day of Silence, the island largely shuts down, including the airport. Check dates if you plan to travel in March or April.
- **Water:** Drink bottled or filtered water.
- **Money:** Use authorized money changers or bank ATMs, and count your cash before leaving.
- **Animals:** Keep belongings secure around monkeys, and avoid touching stray animals.
- **Tipping:** Not obligatory, but appreciated. Many restaurants add a service charge.
- **Safety:** Bali is generally safe. Be cautious when riding scooters, as traffic can be chaotic; an international driving permit and helmet are required. Check official government travel advisories before your trip.

## Getting around

Bali has no rail system, and traffic can be heavy. Hiring a private driver is common for day trips, and ride-hailing apps operate in many areas, though some local zones restrict them. Scooters are popular but only for experienced riders. Fast boats connect Bali with Nusa Penida and other islands. Ngurah Rai International Airport sits in the south, near Kuta.

## Best time to visit

The dry season, roughly April through October, brings sunny days and is popular with travelers, especially in July and August. The wet season, roughly November through March, brings humidity and heavy downpours but lush landscapes and fewer crowds.

## Budget tips

- Eat at local warungs for delicious, inexpensive meals.
- Hire a driver for a full day and split costs with others.
- Stay in guesthouses or villas, which offer good value.
- Travel in shoulder seasons for lower rates.
- Book fast boats directly with reputable operators.
- Visit free or low-cost temples and beaches.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many days do I need in Bali?

A week lets you explore Ubud, the beaches, and an island trip. Longer stays allow for a slower pace.

### Is Bali good for families?

Yes, especially Sanur and Nusa Dua, which have calm beaches and family-friendly resorts.

### Where should I stay for beaches?

Seminyak, Uluwatu, Nusa Dua, and Sanur offer different beach experiences, from surf breaks to calm lagoons.

### Do I need to rent a scooter?

No. Drivers and ride-hailing apps are good alternatives, especially if you're inexperienced.
