## Why visit Cusco

Cusco was the capital of the Inca Empire and is now the main gateway to Machu Picchu. The city's colonial churches and mansions sit on top of finely fitted Inca stone walls, creating a remarkable blend of cultures. At about 11,150 feet (3,400 meters), it is set high in the Andes, surrounded by mountains, terraced valleys and traditional villages. Cusco is a highlight of any trip to Peru, especially for history and nature lovers.

Beyond the famous ruins, Cusco has a lively food scene that puts Andean ingredients such as quinoa, potatoes, corn and alpaca to creative use, along with traditional markets and festivals tied to the agricultural calendar. Many travelers spend at least a few days here before or after Machu Picchu.

## Top sights and experiences

- **Machu Picchu** — the famous Inca citadel, reached by train and bus or by trekking routes.
- **Plaza de Armas** — Cusco's main square, flanked by the cathedral and colonial arcades.
- **Qorikancha** — the Inca Temple of the Sun, later built over by the Santo Domingo convent.
- **Sacsayhuamán** — massive Inca stone walls on a hill above the city.
- **Sacred Valley** — Pisac, Ollantaytambo and other Inca sites and market towns.
- **San Blas** — a steep, artsy neighborhood with workshops and views.
- **San Pedro Market** — a busy market for food, produce and crafts.
- **Rainbow Mountain** — colorful high-altitude slopes, best for acclimatized hikers.
- **Moray and Maras** — circular Inca terraces and salt pans.
- **Inca Trail** — a classic multi-day trek to Machu Picchu, requiring permits and a licensed operator.

## Where to stay: best neighborhoods

**Historic Center** suits first-timers. Pros: walkable to main sights, restaurants. Cons: busy and can be noisy.

**San Blas** works for artsy travelers and couples. Pros: charming streets, galleries, views. Cons: steep climbs at altitude.

**San Cristóbal** fits travelers seeking quieter, scenic stays. Pros: views over the city, close to Sacsayhuamán. Cons: steep and fewer amenities.

**Sacred Valley** appeals to those wanting to acclimatize at lower altitude. Pros: rural setting, closer to Machu Picchu trains. Cons: farther from Cusco's attractions.

## Things to know before you go

- Altitude sickness is common. Take your first days slowly, drink water and consider starting in the lower Sacred Valley. Talk to a doctor before travel if you have concerns.
- US travelers need a valid passport; check official sources for current entry rules.
- Check official government travel advisories before your trip. Watch for pickpockets in crowded markets and on public transport.
- Machu Picchu entry uses timed tickets and set circuits, and rules change; book in advance through official sources.
- Street vendors and photo offers with llamas or people in traditional clothing often expect a tip; ask first.
- Tipping is appreciated for guides, porters and restaurant staff.
- Drink bottled or purified water.

## Getting around

Cusco's historic center is compact and walkable, though steep. Taxis are inexpensive, but use registered taxis or ride-hailing. Trains run from Cusco area stations and Ollantaytambo to Aguas Calientes, the town below Machu Picchu. Shuttle buses then climb to the ruins. Tours and colectivos (shared vans) serve the Sacred Valley.

## Best time to visit

The dry season from April to October brings sunny days and cold nights, with peak crowds in June to August. The rainy season from November to March brings fewer visitors and green landscapes, and the Inca Trail typically closes for maintenance in February.

## Budget tips

- Visit in the shoulder months of April, May, September or October for better rates and fewer crowds.
- Compare train classes to Machu Picchu.
- Eat set lunch menus for value.
- Buy a tourist ticket that covers multiple sites if you plan to visit several.
- Use colectivos for Sacred Valley trips.
- Book Machu Picchu tickets and trains early to avoid premium resale prices.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many days do I need in Cusco?

Four to five days covers Cusco, the Sacred Valley and Machu Picchu, with time to acclimatize.

### How do I get to Machu Picchu?

Most visitors take a train to Aguas Calientes and a bus to the ruins. Trekkers can hike the Inca Trail or alternative routes.

### Will I get altitude sickness?

Many people feel some symptoms. Ascend gradually, rest and stay hydrated.

### Is Cusco good for families?

Yes, with careful planning around altitude and activity levels.
