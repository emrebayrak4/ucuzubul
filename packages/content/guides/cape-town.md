## Why visit Cape Town

Cape Town sits beneath the flat-topped Table Mountain, between two oceans and a dramatic coastline. Few cities offer such easy access to nature: you can hike a mountain, see penguins on a beach, and sip wine in historic vineyards within a day's drive. The city also offers a rich, complex history, colorful neighborhoods, and a creative food scene. It's a popular destination for adventure, scenery, and culture alike.

## Top sights and experiences

- **Table Mountain** — Ride the cableway or hike up for sweeping views.
- **Cape Point and the Cape of Good Hope** — Rugged headlands in a nature reserve.
- **Boulders Beach** — A colony of African penguins near Simon's Town.
- **Robben Island** — The former prison where Nelson Mandela was held; book ahead.
- **Kirstenbosch National Botanical Garden** — Native plants on the mountain's slopes.
- **V&A Waterfront** — Shops, restaurants, and harbor views.
- **Bo-Kaap** — Colorful houses and Cape Malay heritage.
- **Chapman's Peak Drive** — A spectacular coastal road.
- **Lion's Head hike** — A popular sunrise or sunset climb.
- **Cape Winelands** — Stellenbosch and Franschhoek wineries.

## Where to stay: best neighborhoods

**V&A Waterfront** offers convenience, security, and dining. It suits first-timers and families, with higher rates.

**City Bowl** is central, with a mix of hotels, restaurants, and nightlife. Convenient for exploring.

**Sea Point** has an oceanfront promenade, restaurants, and good value options.

**Camps Bay** offers beach views and a glamorous atmosphere. It's pricey and less central.

**Green Point** is close to the Waterfront and stadium, with a range of accommodation.

**Gardens** is a leafy, central neighborhood near the Company's Garden, with a residential feel.

## Things to know before you go

- **Entry:** US citizens can generally visit South Africa for short stays without a visa. South Africa has specific passport validity and blank-page requirements. Check official sources before you travel.
- **Weather:** The wind can be strong, especially in summer, and Table Mountain's cableway may close in bad weather. Check status before going.
- **Hiking:** Hike in groups, start early, carry water, and tell someone your plans.
- **Tipping:** Around 10 to 15 percent in restaurants is customary. Small tips are expected for car guards and fuel attendants.
- **Power supply:** Scheduled power cuts have affected South Africa in recent years. Check current status, and note that many hotels have backup power.
- **Safety:** Like many large cities, Cape Town has areas with higher crime. Avoid walking alone at night in isolated areas, keep valuables out of sight, and use ride-hailing apps after dark. Use ATMs inside banks or malls, and decline unsolicited help. Check official government travel advisories before your trip.

## Getting around

Ride-hailing apps are widely used and convenient. The MyCiTi bus network covers key routes, including the airport, the city center, and the Atlantic seaboard. Renting a car is popular for exploring the Cape Peninsula and Winelands; driving is on the left. Hop-on, hop-off buses cover major sights.

## Best time to visit

Summer, from November to March, brings warm, dry weather and is peak season. Fall and spring offer milder temperatures and fewer crowds. Winter, from June to August, is cooler and rainier, with lower rates and greener landscapes. Whale watching along the nearby coast is seasonal, roughly in the winter and spring months.

## Budget tips

- Hike Table Mountain or Lion's Head instead of paying for the cableway.
- Eat at markets and casual spots for good-value meals.
- Visit free beaches and the Sea Point promenade.
- Travel in winter or shoulder seasons for lower rates.
- Book Robben Island and cableway tickets online in advance.
- Share car rental costs with others for day trips.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many days should I spend in Cape Town?

Four to five days covers Table Mountain, the Peninsula, and the Winelands.

### Is tap water safe in Cape Town?

Tap water is generally considered safe to drink.

### Do I need a car?

Not necessarily, but a car helps for exploring the Peninsula and Winelands. Tours and ride-hailing are alternatives.

### Can I see penguins in Cape Town?

Yes, at Boulders Beach near Simon's Town, home to a colony of African penguins.
