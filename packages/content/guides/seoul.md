## Why visit Seoul

Seoul blends centuries-old palaces and hanok houses with cutting-edge technology, design, and pop culture. The South Korean capital is set among mountains and divided by the Han River, giving it easy access to hiking trails and riverside parks. Food is a constant highlight, from sizzling barbecue to street-market snacks, and the city's shopping, cafe culture, and nightlife run late. Efficient transit and a strong sense of safety make it easy to explore.

## Top sights and experiences

- **Gyeongbokgung Palace** — The grandest of Seoul's royal palaces, with a changing of the guard ceremony.
- **Changdeokgung Palace and Secret Garden** — A UNESCO-listed palace with a tranquil garden, by guided tour.
- **Bukchon Hanok Village** — Traditional houses on hillside lanes; a residential area with visiting rules.
- **Insadong** — Tea houses, galleries, and traditional crafts.
- **N Seoul Tower** — City views from atop Namsan mountain.
- **Gwangjang Market** — A historic market famous for street food.
- **Myeongdong** — Shopping, skincare stores, and evening food stalls.
- **Cheonggyecheon Stream** — A restored urban stream for a peaceful walk.
- **Dongdaemun Design Plaza** — A futuristic landmark and design hub.
- **DMZ tour** — A guided visit to the border area; book with an approved operator.

## Where to stay: best neighborhoods

**Myeongdong** is central, convenient, and packed with shopping and street food. It suits first-timers but is crowded.

**Jongno** places you near palaces, Insadong, and Bukchon, ideal for history and culture lovers.

**Hongdae** is youthful and lively, with indie music, cafes, and nightlife, and has a direct rail link to Incheon Airport. It can be noisy at night.

**Gangnam** is upscale and modern, with shopping and dining, though farther from the historic sights.

**Itaewon** is international and diverse, with a wide range of restaurants and bars.

## Things to know before you go

- **Entry:** South Korea's travel authorization rules for US citizens have changed in recent years. Check official sources to see whether you need a K-ETA or other pre-registration before travel.
- **Map apps:** Some global map apps have limited navigation in Korea. Local apps like Naver Map and KakaoMap work better.
- **Etiquette:** Respect elders, use two hands when handing items, and keep voices low on public transit. Seats reserved for elderly, pregnant, or disabled riders should be left open.
- **Bukchon:** Residents live in the hanok village. Visit during permitted hours, keep noise down, and follow posted restrictions.
- **Tipping:** Not customary.
- **Convenience stores:** Convenience stores are everywhere and handy for snacks, transit card top-ups, and essentials.
- **Dining customs:** Many restaurants have a call button on the table; it's normal to press it for service. You typically pay at the counter on the way out.
- **Safety:** Seoul is generally very safe. Normal precautions apply in crowded areas and nightlife districts. Check official government travel advisories before your trip.

## Getting around

The subway is extensive, clean, and signed in English. A rechargeable transit card works on subways, buses, and taxis, and at convenience stores. Taxis are plentiful, and ride-hailing apps are available. From Incheon Airport, the AREX express and all-stop trains, as well as airport limousine buses, connect to the city. Gimpo Airport mainly handles domestic and some regional flights.

## Best time to visit

Spring brings cherry blossoms and mild weather, and fall offers colorful foliage and crisp air. Summer is hot and humid, with a monsoon season. Winter is cold and dry, but quieter.

## Budget tips

- Visitors wearing hanbok often receive free entry to the royal palaces.
- Eat at markets and local restaurants, where meals are filling and affordable.
- Use a transit card for discounted fares and transfers.
- Explore free attractions like Cheonggyecheon, Bukchon, and the Han River parks.
- Stay in Hongdae or Jongno for good value and transport links.
- Shop for skincare at local stores, which often run promotions.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many days should I spend in Seoul?

Four to five days covers the palaces, markets, and neighborhoods, with time for a DMZ tour.

### Is Seoul easy to navigate?

Yes, especially with local map apps and the well-signed subway.

### Do I need cash in Seoul?

Cards are accepted almost everywhere, though small market stalls may prefer cash.

### Is Seoul good for families?

Yes. Palaces, parks, museums, and amusement parks make it family-friendly.
