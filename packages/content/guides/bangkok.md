## Why visit Bangkok

Bangkok is a city of dazzling contrasts, where gilded temples and floating markets meet glossy malls and rooftop bars. Thailand's capital has one of the world's great street-food cultures, a buzzing river at its heart, and a warm welcome for visitors. It offers excellent value across a range of travel styles and serves as a gateway to Thailand's beaches, mountains, and historic cities. Energetic and sometimes overwhelming, Bangkok rewards those who embrace its pace.

## Top sights and experiences

- **Grand Palace and Wat Phra Kaew** — The former royal residence and the Temple of the Emerald Buddha.
- **Wat Pho** — Home of the giant Reclining Buddha and a traditional massage school.
- **Wat Arun** — The Temple of Dawn on the river, striking at sunset.
- **Chao Phraya River** — Ride the river boats past temples and landmarks.
- **Chatuchak Weekend Market** — A massive market with goods of every kind.
- **Chinatown (Yaowarat)** — Famous for evening street food.
- **Jim Thompson House** — A traditional Thai house museum.
- **Lumphini Park** — A green escape in the city center.
- **Floating markets** — Day trips to canal markets outside the city.
- **Day trip to Ayutthaya** — The ruins of a former capital.

## Where to stay: best neighborhoods

**Sukhumvit** is a long, lively corridor of hotels, restaurants, and nightlife along the BTS Skytrain. It suits first-timers and night owls, though traffic is heavy.

**Silom** is a business district with nightlife, Lumphini Park, and good transit links. It is convenient but busy.

**Riverside** offers scenic views and easy boat access to the temples, often with a more relaxed feel.

**Siam** is the shopping hub at the center of the BTS network, ideal for families and shoppers.

**Old Town (Rattanakosin)** places you near the major temples and has a traditional feel, though it is less connected by rail.

**Ari** is a quieter, local neighborhood with cafes and a residential vibe, convenient to the BTS.

## Things to know before you go

- **Entry:** Thailand's visa exemption rules and digital arrival requirements change from time to time. Check official sources before you travel.
- **Temple dress code:** Cover shoulders and knees, and remove shoes before entering temple buildings. The Grand Palace has strict dress requirements.
- **Respect for the monarchy:** Laws protecting the monarchy are strict. Show respect in conversation and on social media.
- **Tipping:** Not obligatory, but leaving small change or rounding up is appreciated. Some upscale restaurants add a service charge.
- **Scams:** Be cautious of strangers saying a major site is closed and offering to take you elsewhere, or tuk-tuk tours that stop at gem or tailor shops.
- **Safety:** Bangkok is generally safe for visitors. Watch belongings in crowded markets and on public transit. Check official government travel advisories before your trip.

## Getting around

The BTS Skytrain and MRT subway avoid traffic and connect major areas. River boats on the Chao Phraya are scenic and practical for temple visits. Taxis should use the meter; ride-hailing apps are widely used. Tuk-tuks are fun but agree on fares first. The Airport Rail Link connects Suvarnabhumi Airport to the city; Don Mueang Airport handles many budget flights.

## Best time to visit

November through February is the cool, dry season and peak travel time. March through May is very hot. The rainy season runs roughly from June to October, often with afternoon downpours, but with fewer crowds and lower rates.

## Budget tips

- Eat street food and at local markets for flavorful, inexpensive meals.
- Use the BTS, MRT, and river boats instead of taxis in heavy traffic.
- Many temples have modest entry fees or are free.
- Stay in Ari, Old Town, or along the river for good value.
- Shop at markets like Chatuchak for bargains; friendly bargaining is common.
- Travel in the rainy season for lower hotel rates.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many days do I need in Bangkok?

Three to four days covers temples, markets, and nightlife, plus a day trip.

### What should I wear to temples?

Clothing covering shoulders and knees. Some sites offer cover-ups, but bring your own.

### Is street food safe to eat?

Many visitors enjoy it safely. Choose busy stalls with high turnover and freshly cooked food.

### Which airport should I use?

Suvarnabhumi handles most long-haul flights. Don Mueang serves many low-cost airlines.
