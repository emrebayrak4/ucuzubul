## Why visit Singapore

Singapore is a compact, ultra-efficient city-state where futuristic architecture meets lush greenery and a rich mix of Chinese, Malay, Indian, and Peranakan cultures. Its hawker centers serve some of the most celebrated, affordable food anywhere, and its attractions range from world-class gardens to wildlife parks and island resorts. Clean, safe, and easy to navigate, it is ideal for first-time visitors to Asia, families, and stopover travelers.

## Top sights and experiences

- **Gardens by the Bay** — Supertrees and climate-controlled conservatories.
- **Marina Bay** — Skyline views and the iconic Marina Bay Sands.
- **Singapore Botanic Gardens** — A UNESCO-listed garden with an orchid collection.
- **Hawker centers** — Sample local dishes at food stalls across the city.
- **Chinatown** — Temples, shophouses, and markets.
- **Little India** — Colorful streets, temples, and food.
- **Kampong Glam** — The historic Malay-Muslim quarter around Sultan Mosque.
- **Sentosa Island** — Beaches, theme parks, and attractions.
- **Singapore Zoo and Night Safari** — Acclaimed wildlife parks.
- **Jewel Changi Airport** — A shopping and garden complex with an indoor waterfall.

## Where to stay: best neighborhoods

**Marina Bay** offers skyline views and proximity to major attractions. It suits first-timers and luxury travelers, with higher rates.

**Chinatown** is central, characterful, and well connected, with food options for all budgets.

**Clarke Quay** is the riverside nightlife hub, ideal for night owls, though it can be noisy.

**Orchard Road** is the shopping district, convenient for families and shoppers, with excellent transit.

**Bugis** is close to Kampong Glam and Little India, offering good value and a vibrant atmosphere.

**Sentosa** suits families seeking a resort-style stay, though it's separated from the city center.

## Things to know before you go

- **Entry:** US citizens can generally visit Singapore for short stays without a visa, but you'll need to complete a digital arrival card before arrival. Check official sources for current rules.
- **Strict laws:** Littering, jaywalking, and eating or drinking on the MRT can bring fines. The sale of chewing gum is restricted, and drug offenses carry severe penalties.
- **Tipping:** Not customary. Many restaurants add a service charge.
- **Dress for the indoors:** Air-conditioning in malls, transit, and restaurants can be strong, so a light layer is useful.
- **Neighborhood variety:** Each ethnic quarter has its own places of worship. Dress modestly and remove shoes where signs ask you to when visiting temples and mosques.
- **Hawker etiquette:** Locals may reserve seats with a tissue packet. Return trays after eating.
- **Climate:** It's hot and humid year-round, with frequent showers. Carry an umbrella and dress light.
- **Safety:** Singapore is generally very safe. Check official government travel advisories before your trip.

## Getting around

The MRT is fast, clean, and covers most of the city, and buses fill gaps. You can tap a contactless bank card or phone on MRT and buses. Taxis and ride-hailing apps are available. Changi Airport connects to the city by MRT, taxi, and ride-hailing. Walking is easy, though heat and humidity can make shaded routes preferable.

## Best time to visit

Singapore is warm year-round. February to April tends to be drier. The northeast monsoon brings more rain from about November to January, and the southwest monsoon from about June to September. Rain often falls in short bursts, so travel is possible any time.

## Budget tips

- Eat at hawker centers, where meals are affordable and delicious.
- Use contactless payment on public transit rather than taxis.
- Enjoy free attractions like the Supertree Grove outdoor gardens and Botanic Gardens.
- Watch the free evening light shows at Gardens by the Bay and Marina Bay.
- Stay in Chinatown or Bugis for better value.
- Book attraction tickets online in advance for possible discounts.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many days do I need in Singapore?

Three to four days covers the main sights and food. A long stopover allows for highlights.

### Is Singapore expensive?

Hotels and alcohol can be pricey, but hawker food and transit are affordable.

### Is Singapore good for families?

Yes. The zoo, Sentosa, gardens, and science museums are family-friendly.

### Can I drink the tap water?

Yes, tap water in Singapore is safe to drink.
