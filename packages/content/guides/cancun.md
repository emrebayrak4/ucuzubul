## Why visit Cancun

Cancun, on Mexico's Caribbean coast in the state of Quintana Roo, is famous for white-sand beaches and turquoise water. It is one of the easiest international beach trips for US travelers, with many direct flights. Beyond the resort strip, the region offers ancient Maya cities, freshwater cenotes, snorkeling reefs and laid-back islands, plus a downtown where you can sample everyday local life and food.

## Top sights and experiences

- **Playa Delfines** — a public beach with sweeping views along the Hotel Zone.
- **Isla Mujeres** — a short ferry ride to a relaxed island with calm beaches and golf-cart touring.
- **Chichen Itza** — the famous Maya archaeological site, a long day trip inland.
- **Cenotes** — natural sinkholes for swimming and snorkeling, especially along the Ruta de los Cenotes.
- **Tulum archaeological site** — Maya ruins perched on cliffs above the sea.
- **Underwater Museum of Art (MUSA)** — submerged sculptures seen by snorkeling, diving or glass-bottom boat.
- **El Rey archaeological zone** — small Maya ruins inside the Hotel Zone.
- **Mercado 28** — a downtown market for crafts and casual Mexican food.
- **Isla Contoy** — a protected bird sanctuary island with limited daily visitors.
- **Snorkeling and diving** — the Mesoamerican Reef system offers colorful marine life.

## Where to stay: best neighborhoods

**Hotel Zone** suits first-timers and beach lovers. Pros: beachfront resorts, nightlife, restaurants. Cons: can feel isolated from local culture and pricier.

**Downtown Cancun** works for budget travelers and food lovers. Pros: local restaurants, markets, lower rates. Cons: no beach on your doorstep.

**Puerto Juarez** fits island hoppers. Pros: near ferry terminals to Isla Mujeres. Cons: limited dining and nightlife.

**Playa Mujeres** appeals to couples seeking quiet resorts. Pros: calm, upscale, newer properties. Cons: far from the city's attractions.

**Isla Mujeres** is good for a laid-back stay. Pros: relaxed pace, calm beaches. Cons: requires a ferry for mainland trips.

## Things to know before you go

- US travelers need a valid passport; check official sources for current entry rules and any tourist form requirements before you go.
- Tipping is customary at restaurants and for guides, drivers and hotel staff; many resorts include gratuities, so ask.
- Check official government travel advisories before your trip, as conditions can vary by region.
- Drink bottled or purified water and use common sense with street food.
- Be cautious with unsolicited offers at the airport arrival hall, where timeshare sales and transport pitches are common; pre-book transfers.
- Confirm taxi fares before you get in, since meters are not always used.
- Seaweed (sargassum) can affect beaches in warmer months; check recent conditions.
- Use reef-safe sunscreen, which is often required at cenotes and marine parks.

## Getting around

Public buses run frequently along the Hotel Zone and downtown and are affordable. Taxis are widely available but agree on the price beforehand. ADO buses connect the airport and downtown to Playa del Carmen, Tulum and other towns. Ferries go to Isla Mujeres from several terminals. Renting a car is an option for exploring the region, but check insurance requirements carefully.

## Best time to visit

December through April is the dry season with warm, sunny weather and high demand. May and June are hotter with fewer crowds. Hurricane season runs from June through November, with the greatest risk often in late summer and early fall.

## Budget tips

- Travel in late spring or late fall for better resort rates.
- Stay downtown and visit public beaches by bus.
- Compare all-inclusive packages against room-only rates plus local dining.
- Book day trips directly with operators or use public transport for sites like Tulum.
- Eat at local markets and taquerias downtown.
- Pay in local currency rather than accepting dynamic currency conversion on cards.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a passport to visit Cancun?

Yes, US citizens need a valid passport book for air travel to Mexico. Check official sources for the latest entry requirements.

### Is Cancun good for families?

Yes. Calm beaches, family-friendly resorts and eco-parks make it popular with families.

### Can I drink the tap water in Cancun?

It is generally recommended to drink bottled or purified water.

### How far is Chichen Itza from Cancun?

It is a long day trip by road. Many visitors join a tour or leave early to beat the heat and crowds.
