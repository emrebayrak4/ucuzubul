## Why visit Montreal

Montreal is the largest city in the province of Quebec and one of the largest French-speaking cities in the world. It blends European charm with North American energy, from cobblestone streets in the old town to colorful neighborhoods with outdoor staircases. The city is known for its festivals, creative food scene and lively nightlife, and its compact layout makes it easy to explore. For many US travelers, it offers an international feel just a short flight or drive away.

## Top sights and experiences

- **Old Montreal** — cobblestone streets, historic buildings and the Old Port along the St. Lawrence River.
- **Notre-Dame Basilica** — an ornate Gothic Revival church with a dramatic interior.
- **Mount Royal Park** — trails and lookouts above the city.
- **Jean-Talon Market** — a lively market with Quebec produce and specialties.
- **Montreal Museum of Fine Arts** — wide-ranging collections across several pavilions.
- **Plateau Mont-Royal** — colorful row houses, cafes and boutiques.
- **Mile End** — a creative neighborhood famous for its bagel bakeries.
- **Underground City (RESO)** — a network of tunnels linking shops and Metro stations, handy in winter.
- **Saint Joseph's Oratory** — a large basilica on the slopes of Mount Royal.
- **Food classics** — poutine, smoked meat sandwiches and Montreal-style bagels.

## Where to stay: best neighborhoods

**Old Montreal** suits couples and history lovers. Pros: atmospheric streets, boutique hotels, waterfront. Cons: touristy and pricier.

**Downtown** works for first-timers and shoppers. Pros: central, many hotels, Metro access. Cons: less charm than older districts.

**Plateau Mont-Royal** fits travelers seeking a local vibe. Pros: cafes, restaurants, parks. Cons: fewer large hotels.

**Quartier Latin** appeals to budget travelers and festival-goers. Pros: near the Quartier des Spectacles, lively bars. Cons: can be noisy.

**Mile End** is good for foodies and creatives. Pros: bakeries, indie shops, artsy atmosphere. Cons: farther from Old Montreal.

## Things to know before you go

- French is the official language of Quebec. Many people speak English, especially downtown, but starting with "Bonjour" is appreciated.
- US citizens flying to Canada need a valid passport; land crossings have other accepted documents. Check official sources.
- Tipping is similar to the US, around 15 to 20 percent at restaurants.
- Check official government travel advisories before your trip, and keep an eye on belongings in busy tourist areas.
- Prices typically exclude sales taxes, which are added at checkout.
- In some establishments, the card machine will prompt you to add a tip; read the screen carefully.
- Winters are very cold; dress in insulated layers and waterproof boots.

## Getting around

The Metro is clean and efficient, with buses covering other routes; pay with an OPUS card or a transit app. The 747 bus runs between Montreal-Trudeau Airport and downtown around the clock. The REM light rail serves parts of the region. BIXI bike share is popular in warmer months. The city is walkable, and a car is unnecessary for most visits.

## Best time to visit

Summer is festival season, with warm weather and outdoor patios. Fall brings colorful foliage on Mount Royal. Winter is cold but festive, with winter festivals and skating. Spring is changeable as the snow melts.

## Budget tips

- Visit in winter or early spring for lower hotel rates.
- Take the 747 bus from the airport instead of a taxi.
- Enjoy free festivals, Mount Royal Park and many outdoor events.
- Check free admission times at museums.
- Eat at bakeries, markets and casual spots in the Plateau and Mile End.
- Buy transit passes for multi-day stays.
- Bring a refillable bottle and snacks for long days of walking.
- Compare weekend and weekday hotel rates, since they can differ.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need to speak French in Montreal?

No, but learning a few basic phrases is helpful and polite.

### How many days do I need in Montreal?

Three days covers the main sights and neighborhoods. Add a day for Quebec City or the Laurentians.

### Is Montreal worth visiting in winter?

Yes, if you dress warmly. Winter festivals, the Underground City and cozy restaurants make it enjoyable.

### Can I drive to Montreal from the US?

Yes, Montreal is a manageable drive from parts of New England and New York. Bring the required documents for the border crossing.
